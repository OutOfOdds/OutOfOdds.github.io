Cash Game

Chip Value

The chip value is what your chips are worth in money, like 1 chip = $0.25. Every session has one.

You record chips in House Rules: buy-ins, rebuys and cash-outs are all in chips. The chip value turns them into money. With it House Rules works out what a buy-in costs, what each player won or lost, and in the end who pays whom.

How it works

The chip value is two numbers: an amount of money and the number of chips it buys.

  • 1 chip = $0.25: a 400-chip buy-in costs $100.
  • 100 chips = $20: the same as $0.20 a chip, so 400 chips cost $80.
  • 1 chip = $1: chips and dollars are the same number.

A player's result is their chips at the end minus the chips they bought, times the chip value. Buy in for 400, cash out 652, and at $0.25 a chip you're up 252 chips, which is +$63.

Chips never show a currency sign. Money is always in your phone's currency.

The cash box is the money players have paid you, the host, for their chips. It doesn't depend on the chip value: a payment is money, recorded once. See The Cash Box.

Set it for a new session

The New Session form has a Chip Value row. It starts with the club's last cash game, so most nights you don't touch it. In a club's first session it's 1 chip = $1 (one unit of your currency) until you change it. How the form works: Starting a Cash Game.

To change it, tap the amount. The keys take two numbers:

  1. Money first: what the chips cost. The keys have a decimal point, so you can type 0.25.
  2. Tap the arrow, the round button at the top right of the keys, to move to Chips: how many chips that money buys. Leave the chips empty and it's one chip.
  3. Check the line above the keys, like 1 chip = $0.25, and tap the checkmark that's now in place of the arrow.

You need at least one chip. Type 0 chips and the keys say Enter at least one chip. and don't save.

Change it during the session

The example: it's two and a half hours into a night at the Maple Street Game. Five players bought 2,400 chips, everyone paid, so the cash box holds $600. Dave, the host, has already cashed out with 652 chips.

Then Dave notices the session says 1 chip = $0.25. He started it from last week's form, but tonight the chips are worth $0.50.

To fix it:

  1. In the session, tap the More button (the three dots at the top right) and choose Edit Session.
  2. Tap the Chip Value amount.
  3. Type the money, 0.5. The chips can stay empty: it's one chip.
  4. Tap the checkmark at the top right of the keys to close them, then the checkmark at the top right of the Edit Session form to save.

What changes

The new value counts for the whole session, including buy-ins and cash-outs recorded before the change. So every amount House Rules works out from chips follows it:

  • the chips on the table are now worth $874 instead of $437;
  • Dave's result is +$126 instead of +$63;
  • rake and tips, if you take them, are counted at the new value too (see Rake, Tips and Rakeback).

What stays

Payments already in the cash box stay as they were. Everyone paid $100 for every 400 chips, and that's still $600. But at $0.50 a chip 400 chips cost $200. So each player still at the table now owes the difference: Jenna, Tyler and Priya $100 each, Marcus $200 for his 800 chips, $500 in all. Their rows say so, and the cash box shows it under still owed.

Dave doesn't owe anything, because he has already cashed out with a win. His result of +$126 already counts his 400 chips at $0.50, so at the end of the night he gets back the $100 he paid plus his win.

Collect it in the cash box when they pay, or leave it: anything unpaid at the end goes into the transfers. See When a Player Pays Later.

check_circle

The easiest night is one chip value from the first buy-in to the end. Check it on the New Session form before anyone buys in.

If the rake or tips would no longer cover rakeback or tips you've already paid out, the new chip value isn't saved. More about those: Rake, Tips and Rakeback.

A finished session can't be edited, so its chip value can't change either.

Choosing a chip value

Pick one that makes the usual buy-in a round number of chips and of money. A few common setups:

  • $100 buy-in for 400 chips: 1 chip = $0.25.
  • $20 buy-in for 100 chips: 100 chips = $20, or $0.20 a chip.
  • $50 buy-in for 50 chips: 1 chip = $1.

Questions

Do I type the chip value in chips or money first? Money first, then chips.

Why does a player suddenly owe money? The chip value went up after they paid. Their chips now cost more than they paid, and the difference is owed.

And if I lower the chip value? Then players have paid more than their chips now cost. Nobody owes the difference: it counts in their favor, and they get it back when you settle up at the end.

Does the chip value carry over to the next session? Yes. The next session in the club starts with it, and you can change it on the form.

Can a tournament have a chip value? No. Tournament chips aren't worth money, see Your First Tournament.
