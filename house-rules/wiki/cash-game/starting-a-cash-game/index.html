Cash Game

Starting a Cash Game

A cash game in House Rules starts with one short form: the club, the game, the blinds and the chip value (what one chip is worth in money). Most nights you don't change anything on it, because it already has what you played last time. Check it, tap the checkmark at the top right, and the session opens.

This article goes through every row of that form, what each one means and what House Rules fills in for you.

The example night

All the screenshots come from the same club.

  • The club: Maple Street Game, six regulars: Dave, Marcus, Jenna, Tyler, Priya and Sam.
  • Last week: they played No-Limit Hold'em with blinds of 4/8 (a small blind of 4 chips and a big blind of 8), and one chip was worth $0.25. So a 400-chip buy-in cost $100.
  • Tonight: Dave, the host, starts the next session from the Lobby. Nothing has changed since last week.

Open the New Session form

  1. In the Lobby, the first screen of the app, tap the Start Session button at the bottom.
  2. Tap Cash Game. (Tournament starts a tournament instead, see Your First Tournament.)

From the Lobby, the new session goes to the club of your most recent session, and the form's first row shows which club that is. To start it in a particular club, open that club from the Lobby and tap + at the bottom of the club's screen, then Cash Game. Both ways open the same form, with these rows from top to bottom: Club, Game, the blinds, Chip Value and Start.

What's already filled in

The New Session form opens with everything from the club's last cash game: the game, the blinds and the chip value. A line under the chip value says where they came from: As in the last session, followed by that session's date.

If tonight is the same game, you're done: tap the checkmark at the top right. The rest of this article is for the nights when something changes.

Club

The Club row shows where the session goes.

  • With one club, it just shows its name.
  • With more than one club, tap the row to pick another club. The game, the blinds and the chip value then change to that club's last cash game. At the bottom of the same list, New Club creates a club right there.

Game

Pick NLH (No-Limit Hold'em) or PLO (Pot-Limit Omaha). The game is shown next to the session's name, like NLH 4/8, and in the club's history.

Blinds and ante

Tap the Small Blind amount. One set of keys takes all three numbers in a row: the small blind, the big blind and the ante (a forced bet from every player, which many home games don't use).

The round button at the top right of the keys moves you on: an arrow goes to the next number, a checkmark saves. It's a different checkmark from the one at the top of the form, which starts the session. The ✕ at the top left of the keys closes the whole form, so to fix a number, delete it with the key next to 0 instead.

  1. Type the small blind and tap the arrow.
  2. Type the big blind, or keep the one House Rules suggests, and tap the arrow again. The suggestion is the big blind from a recent session of the club with the same small blind. With no such session, it's twice the small blind.
  3. The ante is optional. Leave it empty for no ante.
  4. Tap the checkmark on the keys to save all three.

If the numbers are already right, the keys show the checkmark straight away: tap it and you're done.

Next to the digits, the keys show buttons with the blinds of the club's recent sessions, up to three. Each one says when it was played: last time for the last session, a date for older ones. Tap one to fill in both blinds at once.

Here Dave types a small blind of 5. The big blind becomes 10 on its own, and the line under it says why:

The big blind can't be smaller than the small blind. If you type one that is, the keys tell you and don't save it.

You can also leave the blinds empty. The session starts without them, and you can add them later.

Chip value

The Chip Value row says what the chips are worth in money, like 1 chip = $0.25. House Rules uses it to turn chips into money: results, payments and rake. Money is always in your phone's currency, and chips never show a currency sign.

Tap the amount. The keys take two numbers: first the money, then how many chips that money buys. The money keys have a decimal point, so you can type 0.25. Skip the chips and it's one chip.

Use whichever pair is easier to think about. If $20 buys 100 chips, type 20, tap the arrow, type 100. The line above the keys shows what you'll get, here 100 chips = $20, which is the same as $0.20 a chip.

You need at least one chip: with zero chips the keys don't save.

check_circle

It's easiest to set the chip value before anyone buys in and keep it for the whole night. More about choosing one: Chip Value.

Start

Start is Now by default. That's what you want when the game starts as you fill in the form.

If you're entering a game that already started, or one from another night, tap Now. It turns into a date and a time, and you can tap either to change it.

Start the session

Tap the checkmark at the top right. The session opens right away, with no players yet. Seat them with + at the bottom, see Buy-Ins, Rebuys and Cash-Outs.

The ✕ at the top left of the form closes it without starting anything.

Change it later

Typed the wrong blinds? Open the session, tap the More button (the three dots at the top right) and choose Edit Session.

The same form opens as Edit Session, with the game, the blinds, the chip value and the start time. The club can't be changed here. Tap the checkmark to save.

A finished session, one you've closed and settled, can't be edited. See Closing.

Two sessions at once

You can start a session while another one is still running, for example at a second table. The form tells you about it under the club, and both sessions run side by side.

Your very first session

With no clubs yet, the Lobby is empty and shows Start Session at the bottom. Tap it and Cash Game as usual. The form then says the session Goes to a new club, Home Game. The club is created when you tap the checkmark, and you can rename it anytime, see Clubs and Members.

In a club's first session there's nothing to copy, so the blinds are empty and one chip is worth one unit of your currency, like 1 chip = $1. Change it before you start if your chips are worth something else.

For a whole first night step by step, see Your First Cash Game.

Questions

Do I have to fill in the form every week? No. It copies the club's last cash game. If nothing changed, open it and tap the checkmark.

Can I start without blinds? Yes. Leave them empty, and add them later with Edit Session.

Can I change the chip value after people bought in? Yes, with Edit Session. Results and rake in money follow the new value. Payments players have already made into the cash box, the money you hold as the host, stay as they were.

I started the session in the wrong club. What now? The club can't be changed afterwards. Delete the session from More, Delete Session, and start it again in the right club.
