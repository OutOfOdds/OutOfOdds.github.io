Closing and Settling Up

How Settle Up Works

At the end of the night some players are up, some are down, and some money is already in your hands. Settle up is how House Rules turns all of that into a short list of payments, so that everyone ends the night even. Each payment on the list is a transfer: who pays, who gets it, how much.

You see the transfers in the last step of Close Session, see Close Session, Step by Step. This article explains where they come from, on two nights at the Maple Street Game. Dave is the host: he runs the game, holds the money and plays too.

What goes into a result

A player's result is what they won or lost tonight, in money:

  • The chips they cashed out minus the chips they bought, times the chip value, what one chip is worth (see Chip Value).
  • Plus any rakeback they got, part of the house's share given back (see Rake, Tips and Rakeback).
  • Plus expenses they paid for, minus their expense share, their part of a shared cost like pizza (see Expenses).

Money a player paid you for chips, or got back from you, doesn't change their result. It changes how much is still owed to or by them. That money sits in the cash box: the money players have paid you, the host, for their chips (see The Cash Box).

How the transfers are built

House Rules builds the list in three passes:

  1. From Cash Box. The money in the cash box goes to the players it owes, the biggest amount first, until the box is empty. You hand it out.
  2. Between Players. Players still owed money get it straight from players who lost, the biggest loser paying the biggest winner first. That keeps the list short.
  3. To Cash Box. Whatever a player still owes after that, they pay to you.

The transfers come in these three groups, in that order. A group with nothing in it isn't shown.

Night 1: everyone pays at the end

On this night nobody paid for chips during the game: everyone played on Not Yet, the payment choice for "pay later" (see When a Player Pays Later). The cash box is empty. There's no rake, no tips and no pizza.

1 chip = $0.25, so 400 chips cost $100. Dave plays and wins.

  • Jenna bought 400 chips, cashed out 1,108: +$177.
  • Dave bought 400, cashed out 652: +$63.
  • Priya bought 400, cashed out 548: +$37.
  • Tyler bought 400, cashed out 92: −$77.
  • Marcus bought 800, cashed out nothing: −$200.

The winners are up $277 in all, and the losers are down the same $277.

In Close Session, Collect says 2 players owe $277: Marcus and Tyler. The Cash card shows Should be in cash $0, because nobody has paid you anything. Dave doesn't collect from them now: he taps Continue, so what Marcus and Tyler owe goes into the transfers.

With nothing in the cash box, every transfer is Between Players. Four transfers settle the night. A transfer is marked once you record it as paid, so 0 of 4 marked means none is paid yet.

  • Marcus → Jenna, $177. The biggest loser pays the biggest winner. Jenna is done.
  • Marcus → Dave, $23. The rest of Marcus's $200 goes to the next winner.
  • Tyler → Dave, $40. Dave was up $63 and has $23, so Tyler pays him the other $40.
  • Tyler → Priya, $37. The rest of Tyler's $77.

Five players, four transfers. Going through Dave would take five: Marcus and Tyler pay Dave, then Dave pays Jenna and Priya, and keeps his own $63. Settling directly saves the trips, and the money never passes through the host.

The bell next to a transfer between players sends the payer a reminder, see Who Owes and Reminders.

Night 2: the usual night

This night is the one from Your First Cash Game. Sam joins the five, and 1 chip is still $0.25.

  • The chips: six players bought 3,200 chips and cashed out 3,080. Sam bought 400, $100, and cashed out 224. The other 120 went to the house: 80 chips of rake, $20, and 40 chips of tips for the dealer, $10.
  • The pizza: Dave paid $48, and all six share it, $8 each.
  • The cash box: $700. Everyone paid for their chips except Sam: Jenna $100 by transfer, the rest $600 in cash.
  • Rakeback, part of the rake given back to players: none tonight, so the step says Not distributed.

The results, with the pizza:

  • Jenna: won $177, pizza −$8, result +$169.
  • Dave: won $63, +$48 back for the pizza, pizza −$8, result +$103.
  • Priya: won $37, pizza −$8, result +$29.
  • Sam: lost $44, pizza −$8, result −$52.
  • Tyler: lost $122, pizza −$8, result −$130.
  • Marcus: lost $141, pizza −$8, result −$149.

Sam lost $44 at the table and shares the pizza, so his result is −$52. He hasn't paid anything yet, so he owes all $52. In Close Session, Dave skips Collect with Continue, so Sam's $52 goes into the transfers. Seven transfers settle the night.

From Cash Box

First, the cash box pays out what it holds, $700. What each player gets is their result plus what they paid in: they get back their own money, and their win or loss on top.

Under each name, a line explains the amount:

  • Jenna, $269: Game +$177, expense share −$8, paid in +$100.
  • Dave, $203: Game +$63, paid for expenses +$48, expense share −$8, paid in +$100.
  • Priya, $129: Game +$37, expense share −$8, paid in +$100.
  • Tyler, $70: Game −$122, expense share −$8, paid in +$200. He lost, but he paid in more than he lost, so he gets the difference back.
  • Marcus, $29: Game −$141, expense share −$8, paid in +$200, from Sam −$22.

Marcus is owed $51 in all, but the box runs out first: $269 + $203 + $129 + $70 is $671, so only $29 is left for him. The rest, $22, comes from Sam, and his line says so: from Sam −$22.

Dave is on the list too. His $203 is the part of the cash box that's his, so he just keeps it.

Between Players

Sam → Marcus, $22. Marcus is the only one still owed, and Sam is the only one who still owes. So Sam pays Marcus directly.

To Cash Box

Sam, $30, owed for the session. Sam owed $52 and paid Marcus $22. The other $30 comes to you. Why $30? The results add up to −$30: winners +$301, losers −$331. That $30 is the house's $20 of rake and the dealer's $10 of tips, chips that left the game. The cash box paid out everything it held, the house's money too, so the last player who still owes pays it back in.

When every transfer is done, the cash box holds exactly $30: $20 rake for the house and $10 for the dealer.

Cash and transfers

The cash box pays out $700, but you hold only $600 of it in cash: Jenna's $100 went to your bank. Handing out $700 in cash would leave you $100 short. So pay at least $100 of it by bank or payment app. For example, send Jenna her $269 by transfer: you hand out $431 in cash and keep $169, the same $169 that left your bank on top of her $100. Record it with Mark Paid by Transfer (touch and hold the transfer), so that Count Cash, the check of the cash in your hands, expects the right amount. See Marking Transfers Done.

check_circle

The list is built when you reach Settle Up. If you then change the rake, tips, rakeback or an expense in Close Session, House Rules builds it again.

Questions

Why does a player who lost get money from the cash box? Because they paid for their chips first. Tyler paid $200 and lost $130 with the pizza, so $70 of his money comes back.

Why doesn't Sam pay the cash box all $52? Because Marcus is still owed $22 after the box is empty. Paying Marcus directly saves a step: Sam doesn't pay you so that you can pay Marcus.

Can I choose who pays whom? No, House Rules picks the shortest list. You can record a partial payment, and the rest stays open, see Marking Transfers Done.

Nobody owes anybody. Then there are no transfers. Settle Up shows each player's result, and you finish the session with the button at the bottom.

A transfer looks wrong. See A Transfer Looks Wrong.
