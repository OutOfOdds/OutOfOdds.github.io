Your Data

Back Up and Restore

A backup is one file with everything you keep in House Rules: every club, member, session and tournament. You save it where you like, in Files, in iCloud Drive or on your Mac, and you can bring it back on this iPhone or on another one. iCloud sync keeps your devices in step; a backup is a copy you keep yourself, for the day something goes wrong.

The example club

The screenshots come from Dave's iPhone. Dave hosts the Maple Street Game on Tuesdays for Marcus, Jenna, Tyler, Priya and Sam, and has a few weeks of sessions in House Rules. He saves a backup to Files, then brings it back.

Back up all data

  1. Open Settings, the gear at the top right of the Lobby.
  2. Under Data, tap Back Up All Data.
  3. The share sheet opens with the backup file. Its name has today's date, for example 27.11.2026-HouseRulesBackup.houserules. Choose Save to Files and pick a folder, or send the file to your Mac with AirDrop.

Once the file is saved, Back Up All Data shows the date of your last backup under it.

With Sync with iCloud off, House Rules reminds you when your last backup is more than a month old: the line under Back Up All Data turns to the warning color.

What's in a backup

  • Your clubs, with their house rules and seasons.
  • Members and their tags.
  • Sessions and tournaments, with buy-ins, cash-outs, the cash box, expenses and transfers.
  • Planned games and who said they're going.
  • Your own blind structures and the blind timer.

Settings that live on the iPhone itself aren't in the backup: your payment link, the notification switches and small display choices. Set them again after a restore on a new iPhone.

House Rules doesn't back up on its own. Save a new backup now and then, for example after a big game or before you change phones.

Restore from a backup

  1. Open Settings and tap Restore from Backup, under Data.
  2. Pick the backup file in Files.
  3. The Restore Backup sheet shows what's in the file: when and on which device it was made, and how many clubs, members, sessions and tournaments it has.
  4. Under How to Restore, choose what happens to what's on the iPhone now, and tap the check mark.

Add what's missing or replace everything

  • Add What's Missing is the choice to start with. It keeps everything on the iPhone and adds what's in the backup but not here: a deleted session comes back, and sessions you added since the backup stay. A message says what was added, for example Backup Restored, Added 3 clubs, 41 members and 62 sessions. If everything in the file is already here, it says Nothing to Add.
  • Replace All Data deletes what's on the iPhone now and puts the backup in its place. Use it to go back to the day of the backup, for example after a mistake you can't undo. A blind timer that's running keeps running.

Replace asks first: Replace All Data? It says how many sessions and clubs on this iPhone are deleted, and that with iCloud on they're deleted on your other devices too. Before it deletes anything, House Rules saves what's on the iPhone to a backup of its own, so you can go back. You'll find it in Files, On My iPhone, House Rules, Backups, with BeforeRestore in its name.

On an iPhone with no data yet, Replace doesn't ask: there's nothing to delete.

When something goes wrong

If the backup can't be made, a message says Couldn't Back Up Data. Try again in a moment.

If a restore doesn't work, the message Couldn't Restore Backup says why, and nothing on the iPhone changes:

  • The file isn't a House Rules backup. Choose a file that ends in .houserules. A .pokersession file is one session; bring it in with Import Session, see Export and Import.
  • The backup is from a newer version of House Rules. Update the app, then restore again.
  • The backup is damaged. Choose another backup.
  • The app can't access the file. Save it to Files and choose it from there.
  • Your current data couldn't be saved to Files first. With Replace All Data, nothing is replaced until the copy of what's here is saved. Free up space on the iPhone and try again.
  • Finish the edit in progress. Close the screen you were editing, then restore again.

A backup file can be up to 200 MB. You can also open a .houserules file from Files or Mail and choose House Rules: the same Restore Backup sheet opens.

Good to know

  • A backup is a whole file of your data, with your players' names and money. Keep it somewhere private.
  • A backup is different from sharing one session: a .pokersession file carries one game for another host, see Export and Import.
  • Changing phones? With iCloud on, you may not need a backup at all, see Moving to a New iPhone.
