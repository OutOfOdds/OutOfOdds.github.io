Your Data

Sync with iCloud

House Rules keeps your clubs, sessions, cash boxes and the blind timer the same on every iPhone and iPad signed in to your Apple Account. There's nothing to set up: iCloud sync is on from the start, and your data goes to your own iCloud, not to us.

The example club

The screenshots come from Dave's iPhone. Dave hosts the Maple Street Game on Tuesdays and keeps the club in House Rules, with its members and a few weeks of sessions. He has an iPad too and wants the same club on both.

Where to find it

Open Settings, the gear at the top right of the Lobby. The Data section has the Sync with iCloud switch, a status line under it, and the two backup buttons, Back Up All Data and Restore from Backup. The note under the section says what to do when something is wrong.

What the status line says

The line under the switch tells you how syncing is going right now:

  • Last Synced: Everything is in step. It shows when the last sync finished, for example Today at 9:41 PM or Yesterday at 9:41 PM, and Not Yet before the first one.
  • Syncing: Changes are going up or coming down right now. The first sync of a big club shows this for a while.
  • Not Signed In to iCloud: The iPhone isn't signed in to an Apple Account. Sign in in the iPhone's Settings app. Until then, everything stays on this iPhone.
  • iCloud Is Unavailable: iCloud is turned off for apps on this iPhone, for example by Screen Time, or can't be reached for now. Your changes stay on this iPhone and sync when it's back.
  • iCloud Storage Is Full: New changes stay on this iPhone. Free up space or get more storage in the iPhone's Settings app.
  • Update House Rules to Sync: This version of House Rules can't sync anymore. Update it in the App Store. Until then, new changes stay on this iPhone.

The four warnings come in the warning color, with what to do in the note under the section. Short hiccups, like a dropped connection, don't show up: iCloud retries them by itself.

Right after you turn the switch on or off, there's no status line at all: the note says the change waits until you open House Rules again, see below.

Your first sync

When you turn on a new iPhone or iPad and open House Rules, your clubs and sessions come down by themselves. A club with a long history can take a while the first time, up to an hour or more on a slow connection. You can use House Rules meanwhile; it goes faster with the app open and the device on Wi-Fi. The status line shows Syncing until it's done.

Turn sync off

You can play with sync off, for example on a second iPhone you only use for the timer.

  1. Open Settings and turn off Sync with iCloud.
  2. House Rules asks first: everything stays on this iPhone, and changes from your other devices stop arriving until you turn it back on. Tap Turn Off iCloud Sync, or tap anywhere else to keep syncing.
  3. The change takes effect the next time you open House Rules. Until then, the note under the section says: Syncing stops after you close House Rules and open it again.

Nothing is deleted when you turn sync off. Your data stays on this iPhone, and the copy in iCloud stays as it was.

Turn it back on

Turn Sync with iCloud on again. There's no question this time. If House Rules was opened with sync off, the note says Syncing starts after you close House Rules and open it again, as in the first screenshot. Close House Rules from the app switcher and open it: what you changed on this iPhone goes up, and what changed on your other devices comes down.

What syncs and what doesn't

Synced through iCloud:

  • Clubs with their house rules, seasons and members.
  • Sessions and tournaments, with buy-ins, cash-outs, the cash box and transfers.
  • Planned games.
  • Your own blind structures and the running blind timer.

Stays on each device:

  • Your payment link under Getting Paid.
  • The switches under Notifications.
  • Small display choices, like how seats are drawn.

The timer runs on every device, but only the device where you started it sends the level change notifications, so you don't get the same alert three times.

Good to know

  • iCloud sync is for your own devices. It doesn't share a club with another host who has a different Apple Account. To pass a session to someone else, send them the file, see Export and Import and Handing a Session Over.
  • iCloud keeps your devices in step, but it isn't a backup: a session you delete is deleted everywhere. Keep a backup file now and then, see Back Up and Restore.
  • Getting a new iPhone? See Moving to a New iPhone.
