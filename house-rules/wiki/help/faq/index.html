Help

FAQ

Short answers to the questions hosts ask most. Each one links to the page with the whole story.

About House Rules

How do I start?

Follow Your First Cash Game: a club, a session, buy-ins, cash-outs and Settle Up, step by step. For a tournament, see Your First Tournament.

Which devices does it run on?

iPhone and iPad with iOS 26 or later.

Is House Rules a gambling app?

No. House Rules is a notebook for the game you host: who bought in, what they cashed out, what's in the cash box and who pays whom at the end. There's no playing for money in the app, no betting, and no money moves through it. Players pay each other the way they always do, in cash or by bank.

Do my players need the app?

No. Only the host uses House Rules. Players get their results, transfers and reminders as ordinary messages you send from the app. See Sharing Results and Who Owes and Reminders.

Do I need an account or the internet?

No to both. There's no sign-up, and everything works offline, at the table or in a basement with no signal. You only need a connection to send a message or a file, like any other app.

Where are my clubs and sessions kept?

On your iPhone or iPad, and nowhere else. House Rules doesn't sync between devices and has no cloud copy of its own. The only other copy is your device's backup, in iCloud or on your computer, which includes House Rules like other apps.

Does it work on iPad?

Yes. On the iPad a sidebar on the left holds your clubs, sessions and the blind timer, the blind timer can have its own window, and you can drop a session file right on House Rules. See Timer Window on iPad and Dropping a File on iPad.

Money and chips

Which currency does House Rules use?

The currency of your iPhone's region, the one in the iPhone's Settings, General, Language & Region. All amounts in the app use it. House Rules doesn't convert money: if you change the region, the same numbers show with the new currency's sign.

What's the difference between chips and money?

Chips are what's on the table, money is what players pay. The chip value links them: at $0.25 a chip, a 400-chip buy-in costs $100. House Rules counts the game in chips and turns every result into money with the chip value. See Chip Value.

A player will pay later. What do I do?

When you seat them, pick Not Yet instead of Cash or Transfer under the buy-in. They owe the cash box until you collect, and whatever is still unpaid at the end goes into the transfers. See When a Player Pays Later.

A player paid by bank transfer. Is that in the cash box?

Yes. The cash box counts both, split into in cash and by transfer, so you know how much paper money should be in your hands. See The Cash Box.

Why does a player who lost get money back?

Because they paid up front. A player who bought in for $200 and lost $122 gets the rest, $78, back from the cash box. The line under each transfer shows what it's made of. See A Transfer Looks Wrong.

Why does someone pay a player they didn't play against?

Settle Up balances money, not hands. It works out the fewest transfers that leave everyone even, so any loser can pay any winner. See How Settle Up Works.

Can rake, tips and shared costs be counted?

Yes. Chips left on the table at the end go to rake and dealer tips, part of the rake can go back to players as rakeback, and shared costs like pizza are split between the players you pick. See Rake, Tips and Rakeback and Expenses.

When something looks off

The chips don't add up at the end.

Chips left over are usually rake and tips. Chips short usually mean a rebuy nobody recorded. See The Chips Don't Add Up.

The cash in my hands doesn't match.

Use Count Cash in the Cash Box, then look for a payment recorded the wrong way. See The Cash Box Doesn't Match.

I typed the wrong number.

Right after the entry, tap Undo in the message at the bottom. Later, tap the player's row, then the entry, and type the right amount. See Buy-Ins, Rebuys and Cash-Outs.

Can I change a finished session?

Yes, after you reopen it. Open the session and tap the lock next to Settlement at the bottom. Transfers you already marked stay done. See A Transfer Looks Wrong.

Sessions and devices

Can two games run at the same time?

Yes. Start another session while one is running, and both run side by side.

Does House Rules do tournaments too?

Yes: entries, re-entries and add-ons, bounties, the prize pool and payouts, deals and season points. See Your First Tournament.

Does the blind timer keep going when I close the app?

Yes. It moves to the next level on its own and sends a notification when the level changes. See Notifications and Lock Screen.

How do I send a session to another phone?

Share it as a .pokersession file from the session's More menu, then open the file on the other phone. Both phones need House Rules. See Export and Import.

Can another host take over a running game?

Yes. In the running session, tap More, then Hand Over Session, and send the file to the new host. Your copy locks, so only one phone runs the game. If you need it again, Take Back unlocks your copy, but changes made on the other phone don't come with it. See Export and Import.

Can I see stats for my players?

Yes. Each club member has a total result, sessions played and winning sessions, and you can export them as a CSV file for a spreadsheet. See Player Stats and CSV.

How do I move everything to a new iPhone?

Restore your old iPhone's backup on the new one, and House Rules comes with its clubs and sessions like any other app. Without a backup, share your sessions one by one as files and import them on the new iPhone. Each file brings its players and their results, so members and their stats build up again as you import. The club's color, house rules and seasons don't travel in the file: set them up again.
