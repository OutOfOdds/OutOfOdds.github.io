Your First Cash Game

This guide walks you through one whole cash game in House Rules: you create a club, seat your players, track every buy-in, rebuy and cash-out, and finish with a list of who pays whom.

You'll follow one real-looking game from start to finish. Read it once before your game night, then keep it open on the night itself.

What you'll end up with

By the end of the night House Rules shows you:

  • every player's result in money, for example Jenna +$169 or Marcus −$149;
  • the rake and dealer tips that came off the table;
  • who paid for food and how it's split;
  • a short list of transfers, like Sam → Marcus $22, that settles everything.

The example game

Every screenshot in this guide comes from the same game, so you can check each number against the one before it.

  • The club: Maple Street Game, six regulars who play at Dave's: Dave, Marcus, Jenna, Tyler, Priya and Sam.
  • The game: No-Limit Hold'em, $1/$2. The table uses chips worth $0.25 each, so the blinds are 4/8 in chips.
  • Buy-in: 400 chips, which is $100 at $0.25 a chip.
  • During the night: Marcus and Tyler each rebuy for 400 chips. Sam joins later and pays at the end of the night. Jenna pays her buy-in by bank transfer, everyone else in cash. Priya leaves early.
  • At the end: 120 chips are left on the table. 80 go to the house as rake ($20), 40 go to the dealer as tips ($10). The house is you, the host, and the dealer is whoever deals for the table. Dave bought $48 of pizza, and everyone splits it.
check_circle

Chips and money are never mixed up in House Rules. Chips are plain numbers, like 400. Money always has a currency sign, like $100. The chip value is the bridge between them: 400 chips × $0.25 = $100.

Before you start

  • House Rules on your iPhone or iPad.
  • Your chip value: how much money one chip (or a stack of chips) is worth at your table. If you play with cash-value chips, it's simply 1 chip = $1.
  • The names of the people you play with. You add them as they sit down, so there's nothing to type in advance.

Step 1. Create a club

A club is your regular group of players. It keeps the history of every session, each member's results, and the settings for your next game.

  1. Open House Rules. You land in the Lobby.
  2. With no clubs yet, tap Create Club in the middle of the screen. Once you have a club, you add more with the + next to Clubs.
  3. Type the club's name and pick a color. The color marks the club's sessions everywhere in the app.
  4. Tap the checkmark. The new club opens right away.

A club name can be up to 40 characters, and two clubs can't share a name. You can rename the club or change its color later from the More button (•••) on the club screen.

check_circle

No club yet? You can skip this step. Tap Start Session in the empty Lobby. The session goes to a new club called Home Game, which you can rename anytime.

Step 2. Start the session

A session is one cash game, from the first buy-in to the last transfer.

  1. In the club, tap + at the bottom, then Cash Game. From the Lobby, Start Session and Cash Game do the same.
  2. The New Session form opens. Check each row:
    • Club: the club this session belongs to. With more than one club, tap it to pick another or create a New Club.
    • NLH or PLO: the game.
    • Small Blind, Big Blind and Ante, in chips. When you enter the small blind, the big blind is suggested at double. Ante is optional: leave it empty for no ante.
    • Chip Value: enter the money first, then how many chips it buys. Skip the chips and it's one chip. In the example, type 0.25 and skip the chips: 1 chip = $0.25. Typing $1 for 4 chips gives the same value.
    • Start: Now by default. Tap it to pick another date and time.
  3. Tap the checkmark. The session opens right away.

Every number in the form opens the app's own keys. The arrow on the keys moves to the next number, the checkmark on the keys goes back to the form. A big blind smaller than the small blind shows a red line, and you can't save until you fix it.

In a club's first session, the chip value starts at 1 chip = $1 until you change it. Your next session in this club starts with the same game, blinds and chip value, so next time it's just +, Cash Game and the checkmark.

If another session is already running, the form says so under Club. That's fine: both run side by side.

To change anything after the session has started, tap the More button (•••) in the session and choose Edit Session.

Step 3. Seat your players

A new club has no members yet, so on your first night each player starts as a New Member:

  1. Tap + at the bottom of the session. The New Player list opens.
  2. Tap New Member, type the name and tap the arrow.
  3. Enter the buy-in in chips.
  4. Under the keys, choose how they pay:
    • Cash: they hand you cash now.
    • Transfer: they send you money now, by bank or app.
    • Not Yet: they pay later. You collect it before the end of the night, or it goes into the final transfers.
  5. Tap the checkmark. The player sits down and becomes a club member: House Rules keeps their results across sessions.
  6. The list stays open, so tap New Member again for the next player. Tap ✕ when everyone is seated.

The line above the keys shows the Cost: the chips in money at the chip value. 400 chips at $0.25 cost $100. A club can't have two members with the same name: the arrow stays off and the app tells you why. Use a last initial, like Sam K., to tell people apart.

Each entry shows a short message at the bottom, like Player added, with Undo. Undo removes exactly what you just recorded, the payment and the new member included.

In the example, Dave, Marcus, Tyler and Priya pay $100 in cash and Jenna sends $100 by transfer.

Players who are already members

Once a club has a session, its screen shows a Members tile. Members you've seated before, or added there with Add Member, are listed under Club Members in the New Player list. Tap the name instead of typing it. Members already at the table aren't in the list.

In the example, Sam texted that he'd come late, so once the session was running you added him on the Members tile with Add Member. He joins two and a half hours in and will pay later. By then Marcus has already rebought once for 400 chips in cash (Step 4 shows how), so the table has 2,400 chips and the cash box $600.

On the right, the keys suggest amounts: the usual buy-in at this table, double it and triple it. Tapping a suggestion records it right away.

Reading the session screen

  • On the Table: the chips in play right now, and what they're worth at the chip value. In the example, 2,800 chips are $700.
  • The tiles: buy-ins (all chips bought tonight), cashed out, how many players are at the table, and how long the session has been running.
  • At the Table: each seated player with their buy-ins in chips.
  • Cash Box at the bottom: the money you've actually received, $600 here: $500 in cash plus Jenna's $100 transfer. Sam hasn't paid his $100 yet, so it isn't in the box.

Step 4. Record rebuys

When a player buys more chips:

  1. Tap Rebuy at the bottom of the session.
  2. Pick the player.
  3. Enter the chips and choose how they pay.
  4. Tap the checkmark.

Above the keys, Total buy-ins shows what the player has bought tonight with this rebuy: 800 chips for Tyler. The suggestions add half a buy-in.

A quicker way: swipe right on the player's row and tap Rebuy. It opens the keys for that player straight away.

Step 5. Cash players out

When a player leaves the table:

  1. Swipe left on their row and tap Cash Out.
  2. Count their chips and enter the number.
  3. Tap Cash Out on the keys.

Above the keys you see Bought in, the player's Result in money, and what's Left on table after they go. Priya bought 400 chips for $100 and leaves with 548 chips, which is $137, so she's up $37.

  • A cash-out can't be more than the chips on the table. If you type too much, a red line tells you by how much.
  • Zero is allowed: a player who lost everything cashes out with 0.
  • The last player still seated gets a Whole Stack suggestion: everything left on the table.
  • Someone wants back in? Tap their row under Cashed Out and tap Back in Game.

Recording a cash-out doesn't move any money. If you pay a player out right away, record it with Pay Out in the cash box. Otherwise their money comes in the final transfers, the way Priya's does in the example.

Cash everyone out the same way as the night ends. In the example, the others hand back:

  • Jenna: 400 bought, 1,108 cashed out ($277)
  • Dave: 400 bought, 652 cashed out ($163)
  • Priya: 400 bought, 548 cashed out ($137)
  • Tyler: 800 bought, 312 cashed out ($78)
  • Marcus: 800 bought, 236 cashed out ($59)
  • Sam: 400 bought, 224 cashed out ($56)
  • Total: 3,200 bought, 3,080 cashed out

Fixing a mistake

Tap a player's row. Their sheet lists every entry: buy-in, rebuys, cash-out. Tap an entry to change the amount, or swipe left on it to delete it. Changing an amount keeps the entry's time and payment.

Step 6. Record rake and tips

When everyone has cashed out, the chips players handed back rarely match the chips they bought: some went to the house (rake) and some to the dealer (tips).

In the example, players bought 3,200 chips and cashed out 3,080. That leaves 120 chips on the table.

  1. On the session screen, tap Chips Left on the Table.
  2. All 120 chips start out as rake. Type the rake, 80.
  3. Tap the arrow. The tips get the rest, 40.
  4. Tap the checkmark.

Rake and tips must add up to the chips left on the table. Once they do, the keys say All chips left on the table go to rake and tips and the checkmark saves them. At $0.25 a chip, 80 chips of rake are $20 and 40 chips of tips are $10.

No rake at your game? Put everything in tips, or everything in rake if the extra chips simply go back to the host.

Step 7. Check the cash box

The cash box is the real money you're holding as the host. Tap Cash Box at the bottom of the session.

  • The big number is what should be in the box: $700, made of $600 in cash and $100 by transfer (Jenna's buy-in).
  • still owed: what players haven't paid yet. Sam owes $52: his $100 buy-in, minus the $56 his 224 chips are worth, plus his $8 share of the pizza.
  • rake: the rake in money, $20. The $10 of tips isn't a tile here: it simply stays in the box until you hand it to the dealer.
  • Before You Settle Up: what's left to do. Tap a row to fix it.

Here you can also:

  • Collect money from a player who owes. Tap Sam owes or Collect, enter the amount, choose Cash or Transfer and tap Collect Payment. You don't have to: whatever is still owed at the end goes into the transfers.
  • Pay Out money from the box.
  • Count Cash: count the bills and compare them with what should be in cash.
  • Add Expenses, like food or table rent, with the + in the Expenses card.

The pizza cost $48 and Dave paid for it. Split evenly between six players, it's $8 each. Dave gets his $48 back in the final transfers, minus his own $8 share.

More about the cash box: The Cash Box. More about food and other costs: Expenses.

Step 8. Close the session

Go back to the session and tap Close Session at the bottom. Once everyone has cashed out, it takes the place of Rebuy. It walks you through five steps, in order:

  1. Cash Out: everyone has left the table.
  2. Rake and Tips: the chips left over are assigned.
  3. Expenses: every expense is split between players.
  4. Collect: money players still owe the cash box.
  5. Settle Up: who pays whom.

Each step shows a checkmark when it's done, a number when it's next, or Not needed when there's nothing to do. The button at the bottom always moves you on to the current step.

In the example, the first three steps are already done. Under Rake and Tips you also see Rakeback, for giving part of the rake back to players (not used here), and Kept by House: what the house keeps from the rake, $20.

Below the steps, the Cash card shows what Should be in cash, $600 here. Tap Count Cash to count the bills before you hand any out.

Collect says 1 player owes $52. This step is optional. Tap Continue to skip it, and Sam's $52 goes into the transfers.

Step 9. Settle up

The Settle Up step lists the transfers that settle everything, in three groups:

  • From Cash Box: money you pay out to players.
  • Between Players: one player pays another directly.
  • To Cash Box: money a player pays you.

Each transfer explains its amount. Jenna's line reads Game +$177, expense share −$8, paid in +$100: she won $177, owes $8 for pizza and already paid $100, so she gets $269 from the cash box.

All seven transfers in the example:

  • Cash Box → Jenna, $269: won $177, pizza −$8, paid in $100.
  • Cash Box → Dave, $203: won $63, gets back $48 for the pizza, pizza −$8, paid in $100.
  • Cash Box → Priya, $129: won $37, pizza −$8, paid in $100.
  • Cash Box → Tyler, $70: lost $122, pizza −$8, paid in $200.
  • Cash Box → Marcus, $29: lost $141, pizza −$8, paid in $200, minus the $22 he gets from Sam.
  • Sam → Marcus, $22: part of what Sam owes.
  • Sam → Cash Box, $30: the rest of what Sam owes.

After all of them, the cash box holds exactly $30: the $20 rake you keep and the $10 you hand to the dealer.

The box pays out $700, but only $600 of it is in bills: Jenna's $100 came in by transfer. So pay some transfers by bank or app instead, for example Jenna's $269. Marking a transfer as paid works the same whichever way the money moved.

Mark each transfer as done

  1. When money moves, tap the transfer. The full amount is already filled in.
  2. Tap Mark as Paid. If the player paid only part, type the smaller amount and tap Record Payment: the rest stays open as a new transfer.
  3. When every transfer is marked, tap Finish Session.

The bell next to an open transfer opens a ready reminder text for the player who pays, which you send in any messaging app. Tap a marked transfer again to unmark it.

Someone pays tomorrow? While transfers are still open, the button at the bottom says Close for Now. Tap it and confirm. The session waits, and the open transfers show up in Who Owes on the club screen. When the money comes in, open the session, tap Close Session and mark them.

When you tap Finish Session, the session ends and shows the final results. Each player's result includes their share of expenses: Jenna won $177 at the table and paid $8 for pizza, so her result is +$169.

Winners got $301 and losers lost $331. The $30 between them is the rake and tips, and it's exactly what's left in the Cash Box. The pizza nets out to zero: Dave's +$48 is the money he gets back, and the six $8 shares pay for it. So Dave's +$103 is really +$55 from the night plus his $48 back.

  • Jenna: +$177 at the table, −$8 pizza, result +$169
  • Dave: +$63 at the table, +$48 back for the pizza, −$8 pizza, result +$103
  • Priya: +$37 at the table, −$8 pizza, result +$29
  • Sam: −$44 at the table, −$8 pizza, result −$52
  • Tyler: −$122 at the table, −$8 pizza, result −$130
  • Marcus: −$141 at the table, −$8 pizza, result −$149

A finished session can't be changed by accident. To fix a mistake, open it and tap Reopen (the lock) at the bottom. Transfers you already marked stay marked.

Questions

Do I have to create a club first?

No. Tap Start Session in the empty Lobby. The session goes to a new club called Home Game, and you can rename it later.

How do I add my players to the club?

Seat them. Everyone you seat with New Member joins the club when you record their buy-in. Once the club has a session, you can also open the Members tile on the club screen and tap Add Member. More in Clubs and Members.

A player paid by bank transfer. Is that in the cash box?

Yes. The cash box counts both. Its big number is split into in cash and by transfer, so you know how much paper money should be in the box.

I typed the wrong number. How do I fix it?

Right after the entry, tap Undo in the message at the bottom. Later, tap the player's row, then tap the entry and type the right amount.

The chips don't add up at the end. What now?

If players cashed out fewer chips than they bought, the rest is rake and tips: tap Chips Left on the Table. If they cashed out more than they bought, see The Chips Don't Add Up.

Can two games run at the same time?

Yes. Start another session while one is running. The New Session form tells you about the other one, and both run side by side.

Do I have to collect from everyone before I settle up?

No. The Collect step is optional. Whatever a player still owes goes into the transfers.

Can I reopen a finished session?

Yes. Open it and tap Reopen (the lock) at the bottom. Transfers you already marked stay marked, and the rest is worked out again.
