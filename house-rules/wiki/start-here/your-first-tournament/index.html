Your First Tournament

This guide walks you through one whole tournament in House Rules: you set the price of an entry, seat your players, record re-entries and busts, and finish with a list of who pays whom.

You'll follow one real-looking tournament from the first entry to the last transfer. Read it once before your game, then keep it open on the night.

What you'll end up with

By the end of the night House Rules shows you:

  • the prize pool and what each paid place wins, for example 1st $228 and 2nd $122;
  • every player's finishing place and result in money, like Jenna +$173;
  • the house fee you keep;
  • a short list of transfers, like Sam → Marcus $20, that settles everything.

The example tournament

Every screenshot in this guide comes from the same tournament, so you can check each number against the one before it.

  • The club: Maple Street Game, six regulars who play at Dave's: Dave, Marcus, Jenna, Tyler, Priya and Sam. They're already club members. If yours aren't yet, you add them as they sit down, see Step 2.
  • The price of an entry: a $50 buy-in that goes to the prize pool, plus a $5 house fee that goes to you, the host. So every entry costs $55.
  • Chips: everyone starts with 20,000 chips. Blinds start at 50/100.
  • Re-entry: a player who busts early can buy back in until the end of Level 4.
  • During the night: Sam sits down without paying and pays at the end. Tyler busts early and re-enters for another $55. Then the players go out one by one: Priya, Sam, Dave, Tyler and Marcus. Jenna wins.
check_circle

Tournament chips aren't worth money. A stack of 20,000 chips is just a stack: there's no chip value like in a cash game. A player's result is their prize minus everything they paid in.

Before you start

The cash box is the money you, the host, hold for the night: entries go in, prizes come out. If you play too, add yourself as a player like everyone else.

  • House Rules on your iPhone or iPad.
  • The buy-in, the house fee (if you take one) and the starting stack.
  • A club. If you have none yet, follow Step 1 of Your First Cash Game. Or tap Start Session and Tournament in the empty Lobby: the tournament goes to a new club called Home Game, which you can rename anytime.

Step 1. Start a new tournament

  1. Open the club from the Lobby.
  2. Tap + at the bottom, then Tournament. From the Lobby, Start Session and Tournament do the same.
  3. The New Tournament form opens.

The price of an entry

The top of the form sets what players pay and what they get:

  • Club: the club this tournament belongs to. With more than one club, tap it to pick another or create a New Club.
  • NLH or PLO4: the game.
  • Buy-In: what each entry adds to the prize pool. A club's first tournament starts at $50.
  • Bounty: Off here. With Fixed or Progressive, players also win money for every player they knock out. See Fixed Bounties and PKO.
  • House Fee: what the house keeps from each entry. It goes to the cash box, not to the prize pool. Leave it empty for no fee.
  • Starting Stack: the chips each entry gets, 20,000 by default.

The line under them sums it up. With a $50 buy-in and a $5 fee it reads Players pay $55 per entry. The fee goes to the cash box, not the prize pool.

Every number opens the app's own keys, like in a cash game: type the amount and tap the checkmark on the keys.

The rules of the night

Scroll down for the rest:

  • Re-Entry: whether a busted player can buy a new stack, and until when: Off, Until Level 4, 6 or 8, Until First Break or Until Second Break. The example uses Until Level 4.
  • Add-On: one extra stack per player at its own price. Off in the example.
  • Guarantee: the least the paid places split, however few players come. Empty for no guarantee. See Prize Pool and Payouts.
  • Dealer Tips: Off, Percent or Amount, taken off the buy-ins and kept in the cash box for the dealer.
  • Tables, Draw Seats: random seats at one or more tables. It's on for a new club. With one table at home you can turn it off, as in the example. See Tables and Seats.
  • Blinds: the Pace of the blind timer, the Level Length and how often there are Breaks. Under them you see the first levels and the length of the whole structure. The example keeps Standard: 15-minute levels, a break every 4 levels, starting at 50/100, 12 levels, ≈ 3h 20m.
  • Payouts: Auto. The more entries, the more places get paid: 1 place up to 4 entries, 2 up to 7, 3 up to 15, 4 up to 24, then 5. You can change it later on the tournament's Payouts screen.

Your next tournament in this club starts with the same settings, so next time it's just +, Tournament and the checkmark.

Start Tournament

Tap the checkmark (Start Tournament). The tournament opens and its blind timer starts right away at Level 1. The first time, iPhone asks whether House Rules may send notifications: allow them, and you get an alert when the level changes, even with the phone locked.

The chip under the title shows the timer: Level 1, the blinds 50 / 100 and the time left in the level. Tap it to open the timer full screen. More in Running the Timer.

check_circle

Another timer already running? The form asks A Timer Is Running first. Link to This Tournament keeps that timer and ties it to the tournament. Start Over From Tournament Blinds restarts it with this tournament's structure.

Step 2. Add your players

  1. Tap + at the bottom. The Add Player list opens.
  2. Leave Paid on if the player hands you the money now. The line under it says $55 goes to the cash box now.
  3. Tap their name under Club Members. They're in right away, and the list stays open for the next player.
  4. Someone who isn't a member yet? Tap New Member and type the name, or type it in Search Members and tap Add with their name in quotes. They join the club.
  5. Tap ✕ when everyone is in.

Sam will pay later. Turn Paid off before you tap his name. The line changes to The player owes $55 until they pay in the cash box. Sam plays all the same, and his row shows owes $55 until he pays.

Each entry shows a short message at the bottom, like Dave is in, with Undo. Undo removes the entry and its payment from the cash box.

A club member's name can't be added twice: typing it under Search Members asks you to choose them from the list instead.

Reading the tournament screen

  • Prize Pool: the buy-ins, $300 for six entries. Under it: 6 entries, $30 house fee. The fee isn't in the pool.
  • players left: 6 of 6. avg stack: the average stack in big blinds (BB), the bigger of the two forced bets of the current level: 200 BB is 20,000 chips at a 100 big blind. chips in play: 120,000. running: how long the tournament has been going.
  • Payouts: what each paid place wins right now. Six entries pay two places, 65% and 35%: $195 and $105. Above them, 4 more out to the money counts how many more players must bust before everyone left wins a prize ("in the money"). Tap the card to open Payouts.
  • The Cash Box button at the bottom shows the money you hold: $275, five paid entries. Sam's $55 isn't in it yet.

Still In lists everyone who's still playing, with how many entries they've bought. Tap a player to record them out. Touch and hold a row for its menu: Player Out, Add-On when the tournament has one, and Delete Player for someone added by mistake whose entry hasn't touched the cash box.

Step 3. Record a re-entry

Tyler busts in the first level and wants back in. Re-entry is open until the end of Level 4, so:

  1. Tap Player Out at the bottom.
  2. Tap Player and pick Tyler.
  3. Choose Re-Enter. The line under it says Re-entry closes after Level 4.
  4. Leave Paid on if he pays now: New stack of 20,000, $55 to the cash box.
  5. Tap Save.

Tyler stays in the game with a fresh stack, and his row now shows 2 entries. The prize pool grows to $350 and the house fee to $35. With seven entries Auto still pays two places, now $228 and $122. Here's why: 35% of $350 is $122.50, every place except 1st rounds down to whole dollars, and the leftover goes to 1st.

When the re-entry window closes, Re-Enter disappears from Player Out and a busted player is simply out. The window follows the tournament's own blind timer. If the timer isn't running, re-entry stays open and it's your call.

Step 4. Record players out

When a player busts for good:

  1. Tap Player Out, or tap the player's row under Still In.
  2. Pick the Player.
  3. Leave Out in 6th selected. The app gives them the next place down: the number of players still in.
  4. Optionally, under Knocked Out By, pick who busted them. It shows in their row, like by Jenna.
  5. Tap Save.

The line at the bottom tells you what the place is worth: No prize, 5 players left, or Wins $122. for a paid place. A message at the bottom confirms the bust, like Priya out in 6th, with Undo.

Record the others the same way as they go. In the example, Tyler knocks out Sam in 5th and Marcus knocks out Dave in 4th. Now it's 1 more out to the money: the next player out is the last one who wins nothing.

Jenna knocks out Tyler in 3rd. Then Marcus goes out in 2nd, and Jenna is the only one left: she gets 1st place and the tournament is over.

Undoing a bust

Picked the wrong player? Tap Undo in the message right away. Later, touch and hold the row of the last player out and tap Back In. Only the latest bust can be undone. Undoing the bust that ended the tournament brings the winner back into play too.

Step 5. Tournament Over

When one player is left, the screen changes to Tournament Over, and the blind timer pauses where it was.

Final Standings shows every place with its result in money: what the player won minus what they paid in.

  • 1st Jenna: won $228, paid $55, +$173
  • 2nd Marcus: won $122, paid $55, +$67
  • 3rd Tyler: paid $110 for two entries, −$110
  • 4th Dave: paid $55, −$55
  • 5th Sam: owes $55, −$55
  • 6th Priya: paid $55, −$55

The results add up to −$35, the house fee. Seven entries cost $385 in all: $350 goes back as prizes and $35 stays with you.

Step 6. Check the cash box

Tap the Cash Box button at the bottom.

  • The big number is the money you hold: $330, six paid entries of $55.
  • Before You Settle Up says Sam owes $55 and can stay unpaid until settlement: whatever he hasn't paid goes into the transfers.
  • The house fee works like rake in a cash game: it's money the house keeps.

You can Collect Sam's $55 here if he pays now, or Count Cash to check the bills. More in The Cash Box.

Step 7. Settle up

Go back and tap Settle Up at the bottom. Close Tournament walks you through five steps, in order:

  1. Places: everyone has a place. It's done by itself once the tournament is over.
  2. House Fee: $35 to the cash box. Kept by House shows what you keep, $35, and Rakeback lets you give part of it back to players.
  3. Expenses: Not needed unless you added an expense, like food.
  4. Collect: 1 player owes $55. This step is optional: tap Continue to skip it.
  5. Settle Up: who pays whom.

Below the steps, the Cash card shows what Should be in cash, $330. Tap Count Cash to count the bills before you hand any out.

Tap Continue. The transfers appear:

  • Cash Box → Jenna, $228: her prize.
  • Cash Box → Marcus, $102: his line reads Prize +$122, from Sam −$20.
  • Sam → Marcus, $20: part of Sam's unpaid $55.
  • Sam → Cash Box, $35: the rest of it, owed for the tournament.

The app keeps the list short: instead of Sam paying the box and the box paying Marcus, part of Sam's debt goes straight to Marcus. After all four, the cash box holds exactly $35: your house fee.

Mark each transfer as done

  1. When money moves, tap the transfer. The full amount is already filled in.
  2. Tap Mark as Paid. If the player paid only part, type the smaller amount and tap Record Payment: the rest stays open as a new transfer.
  3. When every transfer is marked, tap Finish Tournament.

Someone pays tomorrow? While transfers are still open, the button says Close for Now. The tournament waits, and the open transfers show up in Who Owes on the club screen. The bell next to an open transfer opens a ready reminder text for the player who pays. More in Who Owes and Reminders.

When you tap Finish Tournament, the screen changes to Tournament Ended with the results. The cash box button shows $35, the house fee you keep.

A finished tournament can't be changed by accident. To fix a mistake, tap Reopen (the lock) at the bottom. Transfers you already marked stay marked. Settlement shows the transfers again.

Questions

Is the tournament saved if I close the app?

Yes. Everything is saved on your phone as you go. Open the club again and the tournament is where you left it.

A player paid after all. How do I record it?

Open the Cash Box and tap Collect, or leave it for Settle Up: an unpaid entry becomes a transfer to the cash box.

Where does the house fee go?

To the cash box, not the prize pool. In Close Tournament it's the House Fee step, and it's what stays in the cash box after Settle Up: $35 in the example.

How many places get paid?

With Auto: 1 place up to 4 entries, 2 up to 7, 3 up to 15, 4 up to 24, then 5. A re-entry counts as an entry. To pay a different number of places or other shares, open Payouts from the card on the tournament screen.

A player hasn't paid. Can they still play?

Yes. Turn Paid off when you add them. Their row shows owes $55 until they pay in the cash box, and whatever is still unpaid at the end goes into the transfers.

Can a player join after the start?

Yes, while registration is open: tap + and add them like everyone else. With re-entry, it closes when re-entry closes, at the end of Level 4 in the example. Without re-entry, it closes with the first bust. After that, the + for new players is greyed out.

What if I don't run the blind timer?

It starts with the tournament, and you can pause it anytime. While it isn't running, re-entry and late registration don't close by themselves: it's up to you to stop offering them.

Can two players finish in the same place?

No. Each bust gives the next place down. If two players bust on the same hand, record the one who started the hand with fewer chips first: they take the lower place.

Can I reopen a finished tournament?

Yes. Open it and tap Reopen at the bottom. Transfers you already marked stay marked, and the rest is worked out again.
