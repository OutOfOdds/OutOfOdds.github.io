Blind Timer

Blind Structures and Templates

The Blind Timer runs a tournament's clock from a template. A template is a blind structure: the levels and breaks in order, with the blinds and the minutes of each. House Rules comes with three ready-made templates, builds your own step by step from a few answers, and lets you set every level by hand.

This page shows the built-in templates, the step-by-step builder, and how to keep, copy and delete your own templates. Starting and running the clock is in Running the Timer.

The example club

All the screenshots come from the Maple Street Game. Dave is the host and also plays. He runs the club on his phone, so in this story you are Dave.

  • The plan: ten players are coming for a long tournament. Everyone should start deep and have time to play, so Dave wants about five hours, 20,000 chips each and 20-minute levels.
  • What happens: Dave looks at the built-in templates, builds his own one step by step, names it Maple Street Deep, and loads it into the timer.

Open the Blind Timer

In the Lobby, the app's home screen, tap Blind Timer. The screen lists the templates in two cards:

  • Built-In: Standard, Turbo and Deep Stack. Each row shows the number of levels, the total time and the number of players it's made for. Standard has 12 levels, ≈ 3h 20m, 8 players.
  • My Templates: the templates you made. It's empty at first.

At the bottom are two ways to make your own: Build Step by Step, where House Rules works out the levels from a few answers, and the + button, which opens a blank template to fill in by hand. Both are explained below.

Look at a template

Tap a template to see it. Dave opens Standard. The Structure card at the top sums it up: ≈ 3h 20m total time, 12 levels, 2 breaks, made for 8 players, a starting stack of 10,000 and a starting depth of 100 BB. The depth is the starting stack counted in big blinds: 10,000 chips at a big blind of 100.

Below it, Levels lists every level with its blinds and minutes, and the breaks between them. In Standard every level is 15 minutes: 12 levels of 15 minutes plus 2 breaks of 10 minutes make the 3h 20m. Blinds are written as small blind / big blind, so 50 / 100 is a small blind of 50 and a big blind of 100. From Level 5, the line under the blinds shows the ante, here ante 400 at 200 / 400.

At the bottom:

  • Use Template loads it into the timer.
  • Customize Copy opens a copy to edit, named My Standard. The built-in templates themselves never change. Saving the copy puts it in My Templates.

Build a template step by step

The builder asks about your game in three short steps, works out the levels itself, and shows the result in a fourth step for you to check. Dave could almost use Deep Stack, but it runs about 5h 30m for 8 players, so he builds his own. On the Blind Timer screen, tap Build Step by Step.

The line at the top shows where you are, Step 1 of 4 to Step 4 of 4. Continue at the bottom goes to the next step, the arrow left of it goes back. Each step's card has a small i at its top right: tap it for a one-line explanation of the step.

Step 1: Game

The Tournament card starts with the Standard template's answers.

  • Players: how many start. Tap the number and type it on the keys that slide up. On the right are quick picks: 6, 8, 9 and 10 at the table. Tap the checkmark at the top right of the keys to keep it. Dave types 10.
  • Playing Time: about how long the tournament should run, from 2h to 6h in half hours. Dave picks 5h.
  • Expected Re-entries: how many re-entries you expect in all. Their chips are counted, and the late levels run longer. Dave expects none and leaves 0.

Playing Time is a target, not an exact total: the builder fits whole levels into it, so the result can be a little shorter. The tournament may also end sooner, when one player has all the chips. Here the builder starts at 3h 30m even though Standard itself runs 3h 20m.

Step 2: Stack and Pace

  • Starting Stack: the chips each player gets. The keys offer 5,000, 10,000, 20,000 and 40,000. The line above the keys shows the Big blind at level 1 for the stack you type, at the depth set right now.
  • Depth: the starting stack in big blinds: 100, 150, 200 or 250 BB. Deeper stacks give more room to play early on.
  • Smallest Chip: the smallest chip on your table. The blinds are made of this chip, so every blind is one you can pay. 25 is the usual one.
  • Level Length: the minutes of each level: 10, 12, 15, 20, 25 or 30. Longer levels make the pressure build more slowly.
  • Starting Blinds: worked out from the stack and the depth. You don't set it.

Dave types 20,000. The depth is still 100 BB from the Standard answers, so the keys show a first big blind of 200: 20,000 ÷ 100.

Then he picks 200 BB for Depth, so the first big blind becomes 20,000 ÷ 200 = 100, and 20 min for Level Length.

The card now shows Starting Blinds 50 / 100.

If a choice can't make a smooth structure, a warning shows under the card and Continue stays off until you change it.

Step 3: Ante and Breaks

An ante is a small extra bet that goes into the pot before every hand, on top of the blinds. It makes the late levels move faster.

  • Ante: No Ante, Ante from Each Player, where every player at the table pays it, or Big Blind Ante, where the player in the big blind pays one ante for the whole table, the size of the big blind.
  • Ante From: the level the ante starts at, Level 2 to Level 12. It's hidden with No Ante.
  • Breaks: No Breaks, or a break Every 2 levels up to Every 8 levels.
  • Break Length: 5, 10, 15 or 20 min. It's hidden with No Breaks.

Dave keeps Big Blind Ante and moves it to Level 6, so the first five levels run without an ante. He keeps a 10 min break Every 4 levels. Breaks count toward the total time.

Step 4: Structure

The last step shows what the builder made. The Structure card sums it up: 13 levels, 3 breaks, ≈ 4h 50m total time and 200 BB starting depth.

The total adds up from the levels: 13 levels of 20 minutes is 260 minutes, plus 3 breaks of 10 minutes, 290 minutes, or 4h 50m. It's a little under the 5 hours Dave asked for, because a structure only grows a whole level at a time.

Levels lists every level with its blinds and minutes. From level 6 the ante follows in brackets: 400 / 800 (800) is a small blind of 400, a big blind of 800 and a big blind ante of 800. Breaks come after levels 4, 8 and 12, every four levels, so one more break falls just before the last level. The last level is 7,500 / 15,000.

Fine-tune the levels

To change a level yourself, tap Fine-Tune Levels. It opens the whole template for editing: its name, Players, Starting Stack, a Summary, and every level with its blinds and minutes in capsules. Tap a capsule to change it. Save keeps your changes, Cancel throws them away. Dave likes the levels as they are and taps Cancel.

If you fine-tune, then go back and change an answer in an earlier step, House Rules asks Rebuild Levels? before Continue builds the levels again, because your fine-tune edits will be lost.

Save and use

Tap Save and Use. A name box opens over the keyboard with My Home Tournament typed. Dave types Maple Street Deep and taps the checkmark. The X closes the box without saving.

The template is saved, and the timer opens with it: Level 1, 20:00 and 50 / 100, Not started. The clock doesn't run until you tap Start. Under the title, Maple Street Deep shows the 10 players it was built for.

To start the clock and run the evening, see Running the Timer.

Pick another template

Before you tap Start, you can still switch. Tap More, the three dots at the top right, then Change Template. Before Start there's nothing to lose, so the timer goes straight back to the list. Once the clock has started, House Rules asks first.

Your own templates

Maple Street Deep is now in My Templates, with 13 levels, ≈ 4h 50m, 10 players. Tap it to look at it, change it with Edit, or load it with Use Template.

Touch and hold your template's row for two more actions:

  • Duplicate makes a copy, so you can try a variation without losing the original.
  • Delete removes it. House Rules asks first: Delete Template? "Maple Street Deep" will be deleted. This can't be undone. Tap Delete Template to confirm, or tap outside the box to keep it.

Built-in templates have no such menu: they can't be deleted or changed.

Make a template by hand

If you already know your levels, for example from a printed blind sheet, tap the + button at the bottom of the Blind Timer screen. A New Template sheet opens:

  • Template Name, then Players and Starting Stack. With a starting stack, the timer can show the average stack and the depth.
  • Add adds a Level or a Break. Set the blinds and minutes with the capsules in the row.
  • Swipe a level left to delete it. Tap Edit above the levels to reorder them.

The rules are the same for every template: the big blind is always twice the small blind, and the blinds must go up from level to level. Save at the top right keeps the template in My Templates.

To start from a built-in template instead, open it and tap Customize Copy. Here is My Turbo, a copy of Turbo with all its levels, ready to change.

Good to know

  • Each template remembers the players and the starting stack it was made for. When it has a stack, the timer can show the average stack as players drop out. See Running the Timer.
  • A club tournament runs its own timer from the blinds you chose when you set it up. See Setting Up a Tournament.
