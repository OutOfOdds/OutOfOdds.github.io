Blind Timer

Running the Timer

The Blind Timer keeps the clock for a tournament: how long the current level has left, the blinds now and the blinds coming next. It moves to the next level on its own, and you can pause it, skip a level or go back at any time.

This page shows how to start the clock and run it through the evening. Choosing or building the blind structure is in Blind Structures and Templates.

The example club

All the screenshots come from the Maple Street Game. Dave is the host and also plays, so in this story you are Dave.

  • The game: tonight's tournament is at Marcus's place. Marcus keeps the money, so Dave doesn't start a session for it in House Rules. He only uses the Blind Timer, and his Lobby has no sessions yet.
  • The structure: the built-in Standard template: 8 players, 10,000 chips each, 15-minute levels. The blinds are the two forced bets that start each hand, written small blind / big blind: the first level is 50 / 100, a small blind of 50 and a big blind of 100.
  • What happens: Dave starts the clock, pauses it for a phone call, jumps ahead to level 5, keeps track of who's out, and stops the timer at the end.

Load a template

In the Lobby, the app's home screen, tap Blind Timer. Tap a template, here Standard, and then Use Template at the bottom.

The timer opens ready to go, but the clock doesn't run yet: Level 1, 15:00, 50 / 100, Not started. At the top is the template's name and the players it's made for, Standard, 8 players. Up Next shows what comes after this level, two rows, a break included when one is next.

Start the clock

Tap Start when the cards are in the air. The time counts down, and the bar under it fills as the level goes by.

The first time you start a timer, your phone asks whether House Rules may send notifications. Tap Allow to get an alert when a level ends, even with the phone locked. If you tapped Don't Allow, the timer shows how to turn alerts on later. See Notifications and Lock Screen.

When the time runs out, the timer moves to the next level by itself and the new blinds show up. A break counts down the same way.

The buttons

There are always three buttons at the bottom: the left arrow, the big button in the middle (Start, then Pause or Resume), and the right arrow. At the top right are, from left to right, the list button for Levels, More (three dots) and Stop.

  • Pause stops the clock, and the level card says Paused. Resume picks it up from the same second. Dave pauses at 14:43 for a phone call.
  • The right arrow moves to the next level at once, with its full time. The left arrow goes back to the previous level.

Jump to any level

The list button at the top, left of the three dots, opens Levels: the whole structure with every level, its blinds, its minutes and the breaks. The current level is highlighted.

While the clock is running or paused after Start, tap a level to jump to it. Before Start the rows can't be tapped. Dave's game started late at another table, so he moves to Level 5. A small box pops up from the row: Jump to Level 5? The level's clock starts from the top. Tap Jump, or tap outside the box to stay where you are.

The timer is now on Level 5 with 200 / 400 and a big blind ante 400: from this level the player in the big blind also puts in 400 more before each hand, one ante for everyone at the table, so nobody else has to pay one.

Restart a level

If the clock ran while nobody was playing, for example during a long argument over a hand, start the current level again. Tap More, the three dots at the top right, then Restart Level. You can also touch and hold Pause.

House Rules asks: Restart Level? The current level's clock starts from the top. Tap Restart, or tap outside the box to keep the time as it is. Dave keeps it.

Change Template in the same menu stops the timer and goes back to the list of templates. Once the clock has started, House Rules asks first.

Keep track of the stacks

The timer can show the average stack: how many chips the players still in have on average. That tells you how deep the game still is. Tap More and Show Stacks. With a template that has no starting stack, Stacks still counts players and entries and asks you to set the starting stack to see the average.

  • Average Stack: all the chips in play divided by the players left. Under it, the same in big blinds at the current level: 10,000 is 25 BB at a big blind of 400.
  • Players Left: 8 of 8 means 8 still in, out of 8 entries.
  • Entries: every buy-in so far, re-entries included.
  • Chips in Play: the starting stack times the entries, 80,000.
  • Starting Stack: each entry's chips, from the template.

If a count is off, tap Players Left, Entries or Starting Stack and type the right number on the keys that slide up.

As the game goes on, tap the buttons at the bottom:

  • Player Out when a player is knocked out. The chips stay in play, so the average goes up.
  • Re-entry when a player who was out buys in again. It adds a player and a starting stack.

Sam is the first one out: 80,000 chips among 7 players is an average of 11,429. Then Kevin goes out: 80,000 among 6 is 13,333. Kevin buys back in at once with a new 10,000-chip stack, so Dave taps Re-entry. Now 7 of 9 players are left, with 9 entries and 90,000 chips in play, an average of 12,857, or 32 BB.

Every tap shows a message at the bottom with Undo, in case you tapped by mistake. To go back to the clock, tap More and Show Timer.

The Stacks view only counts. It doesn't know who went out: the timer has no list of players or money.

Leave the timer running

The arrow at the top left goes back to the Lobby. The timer keeps running there: a card shows the level, the blinds and the time left, here Level 5, 200 / 400, 14:10 left. Tap the card to come back to the timer.

The timer keeps the right time while the phone sleeps, while you use other apps, and even if House Rules restarts.

Stop the timer

At the end of the night, tap Stop at the top right. House Rules asks: Stop the timer? Level alerts and the Lock Screen timer go away. The template stays loaded.

  • Stop and Exit stops the clock and goes back to where you came from, here the Lobby.
  • Restart from Level 1 stops the clock and stays on the timer, back at the start of the structure.
  • Tap outside the box to keep the clock running.

After Stop and Exit, the Lobby card says Blind Timer, Standard, ready to start. The template stays loaded: tap the card, and the timer is back at Level 1, Not started, for the next game.

A club tournament's own timer

When you run a club tournament in House Rules, it has its own timer, made from the blinds you chose when you set it up. Open the tournament, tap More and Blind Timer. It works the same way, and its Stacks follow the tournament's own entries and knockouts. See Setting Up a Tournament.

Good to know

  • Pause before a long break in the game, and the time left stays exactly where it was.
  • The arrows and a jump always start a level from its full time.
  • There's one timer in the app. Loading another template stops the one that's running, after House Rules asks.
