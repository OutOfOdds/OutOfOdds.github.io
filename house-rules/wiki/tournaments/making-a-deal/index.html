Tournaments and Seasons

Making a Deal

Late at night, with the last few players all in the money, someone often says: "Let's chop it." Instead of playing on until one player has every chip, the players agree to split the prizes that are left. That's a deal. House Rules does the math from the chip counts, shows the amounts for three ways of splitting, and pays the deal instead of the places.

The example tournament

All the screenshots come from one tournament at the Maple Street Game. Dave, the host, runs it on his phone, so in this story you are Dave.

  • The price: a $50 buy-in plus a $5 house fee, no re-entry. Ten players, all paid, make a $500 prize pool.
  • The payouts: Auto pays three places: $250 for 1st, $150 for 2nd, $100 for 3rd. How these are counted is in Prize Pool and Payouts.
  • The final three: Jenna, Marcus and Priya, all in the money. Jenna has 95,000 chips, Marcus 65,000, Priya 40,000. It's almost 1 a.m. and everyone has work tomorrow.
  • What happens: they look at the three ways to split, take ICM, and leave $40 to play for. Then they play on: Priya goes out, then Marcus, and Jenna wins the $40.

When you can make a deal

Once everyone left is in the money, here three players for three paid places, a Deal button appears in the bar at the bottom of the tournament. With four buttons the bar has no room for words and shows icons only. Deal is the third icon, a round dial, between Player Out and +.

You can also open it from More (the three dots at the top right) and Make a Deal. The menu item shows up earlier than the button: as soon as ten players or fewer are left and there are prizes still to pay, even before everyone is in the money.

Enter the stacks

Dave taps Deal. The Make a Deal sheet opens with the players still in under Stacks. Ask each player to count their chips, then tap the box next to their name and type the count on the keys.

The line under the stacks checks the count: 200,000 of 200,000 chips in play. If it doesn't match, someone miscounted, or a stack was typed wrong. Recount before you go on.

Under Deal, each player's row shows the prize their place would pay without a deal, like 1st pays $250, and on the right what they get from the deal.

Pick how to split

The switch above the Deal section has three ways to split the $500 left:

  • Even: everyone gets the same. Rounding leftovers go to the chip leader.
  • Chips: everyone first gets the smallest prize still to be paid, here 3rd place's $100. The other $200 is split in proportion to the chips.
  • ICM: by each player's chances to finish in every place, worked out from the chip counts. This is the usual choice in poker rooms, and the one House Rules starts with.

For Dave's table:

  • Even: Jenna $168, Marcus $166, Priya $166.
  • Chips: Jenna $195, Marcus $165, Priya $140.
  • ICM: Jenna $190, Marcus $167, Priya $143.

Chips pays the leader the most, because it treats every chip as worth the same. ICM reasons that Jenna's extra chips don't guarantee her 1st, while even Priya's short stack still has a real chance of 2nd, so it pays the short stacks a little more. Even is for friends who just want to go home.

The three players agree on ICM.

Leave some to play for

Often players split most of the money but keep a bit to play for, so the game still has a winner. Tap Leave to Play For and type the amount. It can be up to the gap between 1st and 2nd place, here $100, so the player who finishes 2nd never gets less than 2nd place would pay.

Dave types $40. Now the ICM split works on the $460 left, and the note says $40 goes to the winner. Keep playing and mark players out as usual. Jenna gets $171, Marcus $154, Priya $135, $460 in all. Whoever wins also gets the $40 on top.

If you leave nothing to play for, the deal ends the tournament right away: the screen turns into Tournament Over, and places go by chip count, the biggest stack 1st.

Make the deal

To save the deal, tap the checkmark button at the top right of the sheet. Its name is Make Deal. The tournament screen now shows a Deal card in place of Payouts: the ICM deal with each player's amount, and Play For, to the winner, $40.

Payouts now opens with the deal at the top. The places below are marked Places Before the Deal, and Places Paid and percents are locked while the deal stands.

Play for the rest

The three play on for the $40. Priya goes out first. Dave records it with Player Out as usual. The note under the sheet says what she gets: Gets $135 from the deal.

Then Marcus goes out, and Jenna wins. After a deal, the order players go out in doesn't change anyone's amount. Only the winner gets the $40. Jenna's prize is her $171 plus the $40: Jenna wins $211.

Each row in Final Standings says deal instead of won. Paid is the $55 each player paid, buy-in plus house fee. The seven players who went out before the deal are listed below Priya, from 4th to 10th. From here you settle as usual with Settle Up. See Prize Pool and Payouts.

Cancel a deal

Changed your mind, or typed a stack wrong? Tap More and Cancel Deal, or Cancel Deal at the top of Payouts. House Rules asks first. Everyone in the deal goes back in to play, even a player who went out after the deal, and payouts go back to places. Then make the deal again with the right numbers.

Good to know

  • A deal splits only what's still to be paid. Prizes of players already out don't change.
  • Bounties stay outside the deal. Each player keeps the bounties they've won. See Fixed Bounties and PKO.
  • A deal works for 2 to 10 players.
  • The deal is a record of what the players agreed. If they want different amounts, for example to round them, change the stacks or the method until the numbers match what they said.
