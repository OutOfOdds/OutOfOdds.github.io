Tournaments and Seasons

Prize Pool and Payouts

The prize pool is the money the winners share. House Rules builds it from the buy-ins and add-ons, takes out any dealer tips, and tops it up to the guarantee if there is one. Then it splits the pool between the paid places, the payouts.

This page explains where every dollar comes from, how to change the number of paid places and their shares, and what happens when the buy-ins don't reach the guarantee.

The example tournament

All the screenshots come from one tournament at the Maple Street Game. Dave, the host, runs it on his phone, so in this story you are Dave.

  • The price: a $50 buy-in plus a $5 house fee, $55 an entry, no re-entry.
  • The promise: Dave announced a $600 guarantee, a prize pool of at least $600 however many people come.
  • Dealer tips: 3% of the buy-ins, for the friend who deals all night.
  • What happened: only ten players came, all paid. Jenna won, Marcus came 2nd, Dave 3rd. Now Dave checks the payouts before he pays everyone.

How the price, guarantee and tips are set is in Setting Up a Tournament.

Where the money comes from

The tournament is over and the screen shows Final Standings. Jenna wins $300, and the card at the top says the pool is $600, even though ten $50 buy-ins make only $500. The difference is the guarantee.

The numbers on the right are each player's result, prize minus what they paid. Dave's +$65 doesn't count the money he adds for the guarantee, see below.

To see the whole picture, open Payouts. From top to bottom it has Places Paid and Round To, the Places, then Guarantee and Dealer Tips, and last Where the Money Goes. To open it: during the tournament tap the Payouts card, or at any time tap More (the three dots at the top right) and choose Payouts. Scroll down to Where the Money Goes.

Read it from the top:

  • 10 entries × $50, $500: the buy-ins, without the house fee.
  • Dealer Tips 3%, −$15: the tips come off the top. The $15 stays in the cash box for the dealer.
  • Guarantee, +$115: what's missing to reach $600. This gap is called the overlay.
  • Prize Pool, $600: what the places share.
  • House Fee 10 × $5, $50: the house fee is the host's money, here Dave's. It was never part of the $500, and it pays the overlay first.
  • From You, $65: the rest of the overlay, $115 − $50, comes out of Dave's own pocket.

So the guarantee cost Dave his $50 house fee and $65 more. Tips come off before the guarantee is checked, so they make the overlay bigger. With thirteen players, $650 of buy-ins minus $19.50 in tips would clear $600 on their own, and the Guarantee line would disappear.

Add-ons count toward the pool the same way as buy-ins. Re-entries count as entries.

How the places split the pool

At the top of Payouts:

  • Places Paid is how many players get a prize. Auto (3) means House Rules picks the number from the entries: Auto pays 1 place up to 4 entries, 2 up to 7, 3 up to 15, 4 up to 24, then 5. Ten entries pay three places.
  • Round To keeps the prizes in round numbers: $0.01, $1, $5 or $10.
  • Places lists each place with its share and its prize. Here 1st gets 50%, $300, 2nd 30%, $180, and 3rd 20%, $120.

Pay more places

Priya finished 4th and the table thinks she deserves her money back. Dave taps Places Paid and picks 4.

House Rules splits the pool between four places with its standard shares: 45%, 27%, 17% and 11%. That's $270, $162, $102 and $66, still $600 in all.

Change a share

The shares are buttons. Tap one, for example 1st place's 45%, and type the new share on the keys. Tap the checkmark to set it.

The shares must add up to exactly 100%. While they don't, the prizes disappear from the list and the note under the places says how far off you are, for example Percents add up to 95%. Make it 100% to save them. Change another share until the total is 100%, and the new split saves.

To go back to the standard split, choose Auto in Places Paid. Auto sets both the number of places and their shares, so here it goes back to three places.

Rounding

Every place except 1st rounds down to the Round To amount, and whatever is left over goes to 1st. For example, with four places and Round To $10, 2nd gets $160 instead of $162, 3rd $100, 4th $60, and 1st gets the rest, $280. Pick $0.01 to pay exact amounts.

Guarantee and dealer tips

Below the places are the Guarantee, Dealer Tips and the tips amount. You can change them during the tournament and after it, until you settle.

  • Guarantee: tap the amount and type a new one. Zero means no guarantee.
  • Dealer Tips: Off for no tips, Percent for a share of the buy-ins, up to 20%, or Amount for a fixed sum for the whole tournament. Tips come out of the prize pool and stay in the cash box for the dealer.

The note under them sums up the rule: The house fee covers a guarantee shortfall first, you add the rest. Dealer tips come off the top and go to the cash box for the dealer.

Add your part when you close

When the guarantee needs money from you, the tournament can't be settled until you put it in the cash box. Dave taps Settle Up on the Tournament Over screen. Close Tournament opens with its steps: Places checks everyone has a place, House Fee settles the guarantee, Expenses is for costs like food, Collect gets in money still owed, and Settle Up pays the prizes. Here House Fee says Add $65 for the guarantee. The steps are explained in Close Session, Step by Step.

Dave puts $65 of his own cash into the box with the rest of the money, then taps Add $65 so the app records it. House Rules never moves real money, it only keeps track. The message says Added $65 for the guarantee, the step is checked, and From Cash Box lists the prizes to pay: Jenna $300, Marcus $180 and, further down the list, Dave $120.

Check the sums: the box held $550 from ten $55 entries. With Dave's $65 it holds $615. Out of that go the $600 in prizes and the $15 dealer tips. Nothing is left, because the house fee went to the overlay. Counting his $65, Dave's real result for the night is $120 won minus $55 paid minus $65 added, so he breaks even.

Paying the prizes and marking them done is in Close Session, Step by Step.

Good to know

  • Rounding leftovers always go to 1st place, so the prizes add up to the whole pool.
  • Auto pays at most five places. For more, pick the number yourself, up to 10.
  • After a deal, Places Paid and the shares are locked, and the deal amounts are paid instead. See Making a Deal.
  • Bounties are paid on top of the prizes and aren't part of the prize pool. See Fixed Bounties and PKO.
  • The house fee belongs to the house. When there's no overlay to cover, it's yours to keep.
