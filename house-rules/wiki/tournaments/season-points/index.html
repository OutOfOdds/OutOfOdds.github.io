Tournaments and Seasons

How Season Points Work

A season turns your club's tournaments into a league. Every finish scores points, the points add up across the season, and the club sees a table of who's ahead. House Rules takes each player's finishing place from every tournament you ran in the app and turns it into points. You never type a point yourself.

This page shows how to start a season, read the standings, and choose how points are scored.

The example club

All the screenshots come from the Maple Street Game. Dave is the host and also plays. He runs the club on his phone, so in this story you are Dave.

  • The tournaments: five since late October, all with a $50 buy-in plus a $5 house fee, seven or eight players each. All five are settled.
  • The winners: Marcus won three, Jenna one, and Dave won the latest, on Sunday, November 22.
  • What happens: the players want to know who had the best autumn. Dave starts the 2026 Season, looks at the standings, compares the two ways to score, and opens the latest tournament to see its points.

Start a season

Open your club and tap More, the three dots at the top right, then New Season.

A name box opens with 2026 Season already typed. Keep it or type your own. The note under it says what you get: the season runs from Jan 1 to Dec 31 of this year, and scores 10, 7, 5, 3, 2 points for the top five places and 1 for playing. You can change all of that later, for example shorter dates for a fall league. Tap the checkmark.

The season counts every tournament the club has settled within its dates, including those played before you created it. Dave's five autumn tournaments are all in 2026, so they score at once.

Read the standings

The season screen opens right away. At the top is the headline, Dave leads by 4 points, and how much the season covers: 5 tournaments, 8 players, the eight different people who played at least once.

Standings lists every player who played at least one tournament, with how many they played and won, and their points. Dave played all five and finished 2nd three times. Here is how his 41 adds up with the default points:

  • 2nd place, three times: 7 + 7 + 7 = 21
  • 3rd place once: 5
  • 1st place once: 10
  • 1 point for playing, five times: 5
  • In all: 21 + 5 + 10 + 5 = 41

Marcus won three times, 11 points each with the point for playing, and finished 4th in the latest tournament, 3 + 1 = 4. That's 37. He missed one night, and a missed night scores nothing, so Dave is 4 points ahead.

If a player missing nights shouldn't fall behind like Marcus did, count only each player's best results. See Counting Only the Best Results.

Below the standings, Tournaments lists the season's tournaments, newest first, with the number of players and the winner. Tap one to open it.

To send the table to your players, tap the share button at the top right, the square with an arrow, left of More. Your phone's share sheet opens, so you can send it to a group chat or any other app. It has the places and points as text, with no money.

Choose how points are scored

On the season screen, tap More, the three dots at the top right, and Points Rules.

The switch at the top picks one of two ways to score a finish: By Place or By Field. Tap one to see its settings. The checkmark at the top right keeps your changes, the X at the top left throws them away.

By Place

By Place is the default. You set the points for each place yourself under Points for Places: here 10 for 1st, 7 for 2nd, 5 for 3rd, 3 for 4th and 2 for 5th. Tap a number, type the new points on the keys that slide up, and tap their checkmark. Add Place adds the next place, up to 10. Places further down score nothing for the place itself.

By Place suits a club where about the same number of people come every time. A win is worth the same whether seven or eight players came.

By Field

By Field gives a point for every player you outlast, plus one. In a tournament of 12 players, 1st gets 12 and last gets 1. The point for playing is added on top. So a bigger tournament is worth more, and every place scores something.

For Dave's club, By Field would change the order behind him. Dave would have 38 points: 8 for 2nd of 8 players, 7 for 2nd of 7, 6 for 3rd of 7, 8 for 2nd of 8 and 9 for 1st of 8, each with the point for playing. Jenna would have 34 and Marcus 32: Jenna's five steady finishes would count for more than Marcus's wins in four tournaments. Dave only looks and taps the X, so nothing changes.

Points for everyone

Under the places, the same in both ways of scoring:

  • For Playing: points for every tournament a player plays, to reward showing up. The default is 1.
  • Per Knockout: points for every player a player knocks out. The row is hidden until your club has played a bounty tournament, where every knockout is recorded. See Fixed Bounties and PKO.
  • Best Results Count: how many of each player's tournaments count. All counts every one. See Counting Only the Best Results.

House Rules counts the points again from the places every time you open the season. So a change in Points Rules recounts the whole season at once, past tournaments too.

Points in each tournament

Every tournament in the season shows its points. Dave opens the latest one, from Tournaments on the season screen. In Final Standings, each player's row now ends with their points: Dave 11 pts for 1st (10 for the place and 1 for playing), Jenna 8, Priya 6, Marcus 4, Tyler 3, and 1 pt each for Kevin, Sam and Rosa, who finished below 5th.

The season on the club screen

Back on the club screen, a 2026 Season card now shows the top three, with 5 tournaments at the top right. Tap the card to open the season.

What scores and what doesn't

  • Only the club's tournaments score. Cash games don't.
  • A tournament scores once it's settled: you finished Settle Up, the last step where prizes are paid, and closed the tournament. A tournament still running or waiting to be settled doesn't count yet. See Close Session, Step by Step.
  • It must start within the season's dates. To change them, tap More and Season Dates on the season screen.
  • A player who re-enters still finishes in one place, so a tournament is one result for them.

Good to know

  • Ties in the standings go to more wins, then to the better place in the latest tournament.
  • The headline says leads on wins when the top two have the same points.
  • Renaming a season or changing its dates never changes any tournament. To end or delete a season, tap More on the season screen. Both are explained in Counting Only the Best Results.
