Tournaments and Seasons

Setting Up a Tournament

A tournament in House Rules starts from one sheet, New Tournament. In it you set the whole price of an entry and the rules of the night: how much goes to the prize pool and how much to the house, whether busted players can buy back in, the add-on, the guarantee, dealer tips, the tables and the blinds.

Two words you'll see everywhere:

  • The prize pool is the money the places split at the end. Buy-ins and add-ons go there, less any dealer tips.
  • The cash box is the money players have paid you, the host. House fees and dealer tips stay in it. See The Cash Box.

The example tournament

All the screenshots come from one tournament at the Maple Street Game.

  • The club: the Maple Street Game usually plays cash, but once a month Dave, the host, runs a tournament. He expects about twelve players, so two tables.
  • The price: a $50 buy-in for the prize pool and a $5 house fee for the club's cards and snacks, so $55 an entry. Everyone starts with 20,000 chips.
  • The rules: players who bust can re-enter until the first break, and anyone still in can take one $20 add-on for 10,000 more chips.
  • The money on top: Dave promises the places at least $600, and 3% of the buy-ins goes to the dealer.
  • Tables and blinds: seats are drawn at tables of 6, the blinds go up every 20 minutes.

In this story you are Dave.

Open New Tournament

  1. Open your club.
  2. Tap + and choose Tournament.

The sheet opens with sensible starting values: a $50 buy-in, 20,000 chips, no house fee, no re-entry, no add-on, no guarantee and no tips. The club is already chosen at the top, and the game is NLH, No-Limit Hold'em. Tap PLO if you play Pot-Limit Omaha.

Tap a row to change it. A row with an amount opens the same keys at the bottom of the screen: type the number and tap the checkmark on the keys. Above the digits there's a short line on what the amount is for. A row with arrows next to its value opens a short menu.

The price of an entry

The first card holds everything a player pays to get in.

Buy-In is what each entry adds to the prize pool. Dave keeps $50.

House Fee is what the house keeps from each entry. It goes to the cash box, not the prize pool. Leave it at zero if you don't take a fee.

Under the card, a note sums up what a player pays. With the fee it says Players pay $55 per entry. The fee goes to the cash box, not the prize pool.

Starting Stack is the chips each entry gets. Chips never show a dollar sign, they're only for play.

Bounty puts money on every player's head. Players pay it on top of the buy-in and the fee: Off, Fixed or Progressive. Dave's tournament has none. Bounties have their own article, Fixed Bounties and PKO.

Re-entry and the add-on

Re-Entry decides whether a player who busts can pay again and come back with a fresh stack. Tap the row and choose how long that stays possible:

  • Off: one entry per player.
  • Until Level 4, Until Level 6 or Until Level 8.
  • Until First Break or Until Second Break.

Dave picks Until First Break. A re-entry costs the same as an entry, $55 here.

Add-On gives each player one extra stack at its own price. Turn it on and two rows appear:

  • Add-On Price: what the add-on costs. All of it goes to the prize pool, there's no house fee on an add-on.
  • Add-On Chips: the chips it gives.

When you turn it on, the price starts at the buy-in and the chips at half the starting stack. Dave changes the price to $20 and keeps 10,000 chips. The note under the card reminds you: One add-on per player.

Recording entries, re-entries and add-ons during the night is in Entries, Re-Entries and Add-Ons.

Guarantee and dealer tips

Guarantee is the least the places split. If the buy-ins come up short, the house fee covers the gap first and you add the rest. Leave it at zero for no guarantee.

Dealer Tips sets aside money for the dealer. It comes off the top of the buy-ins and stays in the cash box for the dealer:

  • Off: no tips.
  • Percent: a share of the buy-ins, from 1% to 20%. It starts at 3%.
  • Amount: one sum for the whole tournament.

Dave picks Percent and keeps 3%. The note under the card sums up both rules: The house fee covers a guarantee shortfall first, you add the rest. Dealer tips come off the top of the buy-ins.

How the guarantee and tips change the money the places split is worked through in Prize Pool and Payouts.

Tables

The Tables card is for a tournament where House Rules hands out seats.

  • Draw Seats: on, and House Rules gives players random seats when you draw them. Late entries go to the shortest table. Turn it off if you seat people yourself, and the other two rows disappear.
  • Seats per Table: from 2 to 10. It starts at 9.
  • Tables: Auto opens as many tables as the players need, or pick a number from 1 to 8.

Dave's table seats six, so he sets Seats per Table to 6 and leaves Tables on Auto.

Drawing seats and moving players during the night is in Tables and Seats.

Blinds

The Blinds card sets the tournament's blind timer:

  • Pace: the blind structure. Standard is a good start, Turbo goes up faster, Deep Stack slower. Structures you made yourself in the Blind Timer are in the same menu, see Blind Structures and Templates.
  • Level Length: minutes per level, from 1 to 180. The keys suggest 10, 15, 20 and 30.
  • Breaks: None, or a break every 3, 4, 5 or 6 levels.

Under the card you see the first levels, how many levels the pace has and how long the whole structure runs, breaks included. Standard has 12 levels. Dave sets Level Length to 20 min and keeps a break every 4 levels: 12 levels of 20 minutes plus two breaks, ≈ 4h 20m.

Running the timer during play is covered in Running the Timer.

Payouts

The last row, Payouts, says Auto. Auto pays more places as more players enter: 1 place up to 4 entries, 2 up to 7, 3 up to 15, 4 up to 24, then 5. You change places and percents later, on the tournament's Payouts screen.

Start the tournament

Tap the checkmark at the top right. If something is missing, a line in the sheet says what, like Set the buy-in., and the tournament doesn't start until you fix it.

The tournament opens at once, and its blind timer is already running at Level 1. There are no players yet, so nobody has paid into the prize pool and the whole guarantee would come from you: $600 guaranteed, $600 from you. Every entry's buy-in, and then the house fees, cut the part from you, until the prize pool reaches $600 on its own.

Now add the players with +. See Entries, Re-Entries and Add-Ons.

Change the money later

The guarantee, dealer tips and payouts can still change after the start. Tap the More button (three dots) at the top right and choose Payouts.

The Payouts screen has the places, Guarantee, Dealer Tips and Tips, %, and at the bottom Where the Money Goes: what the guarantee adds, the prize pool and what comes from you. The screen calls the gap between the buy-ins and the guarantee the overlay.

Entries you've already recorded keep the price they were paid at. The Payouts screen is explained in full in Prize Pool and Payouts.

The next tournament starts the same

House Rules remembers the club's last tournament. Next month, when Dave taps + and Tournament again, the sheet opens with the same buy-in, fee, re-entry, add-on, guarantee, tips, tables and blinds, and the same game.

Good to know

  • The house fee and dealer tips never go into the prize pool. Both stay in the cash box.
  • The add-on pays no house fee: all of it goes to the prize pool.
  • Re-entry closes by the tournament's own blind timer. If the timer isn't running, re-entry stays open and it's your call.
  • To close the sheet without starting anything, tap the cross at the top left.
