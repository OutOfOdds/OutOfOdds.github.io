Something Looks Off

A Transfer Looks Wrong

Settle Up balances money, not hands. It works out the fewest transfers that leave everyone even, so a transfer can look odd: someone pays a player they never played a hand against, or an amount isn't the result you remember. Most of the time the transfer is right, and the line under it says why.

This page explains how to read a transfer, what changes the plan, and how to fix a real mistake, even after the session is finished.

The example club

All the screenshots come from the Maple Street Game, Dave's home game on Maple Street.

  • The club: Dave hosts for Marcus, Jenna, Tyler, Priya and Sam. They play No-Limit Hold'em at 4/8 blinds with a chip worth $0.25, so a 400-chip buy-in costs $100.
  • Tonight: everyone has cashed out. Marcus and Tyler rebought once. 80 chips went to rake and 40 to dealer tips, $30 in all, so the players' results add up to −$30, not zero. Dave paid $48 for pizza, split evenly, $8 each.
  • Payments: everyone paid the cash box for their buy-ins and rebuys, Jenna by bank, except Sam. He lost 176 chips (−$44) and owes $52 with his pizza share.
  • What happens: Dave settles up. Sam pays part of what he owes, and the plan changes under Dave's eyes.

Read the line under a transfer

The screenshots show the Settle Up step of Close Session. It lists the transfers in three groups: From Cash Box, Between Players and To Cash Box. Under each name a line adds up the amount, part by part:

  • Game: the player's result at the table, chips at chip value.
  • Rakeback: their share of the rakeback.
  • Paid for expenses: what they paid for the group, like Dave's pizza.
  • Expense share: their part of the expenses.
  • Short chips: their share of chips that went missing, see The Chips Don't Add Up.
  • Paid in: what they've already put into the cash box.
  • Taken from cash box: what they've already taken out of it.

Parts that are zero are left out. Gains have a plus, costs a minus.

Take Tyler. He lost $122 at the table and had $8 of pizza, so his night cost him $130. But he paid $200 into the cash box for his buy-in and rebuy. So the cash box gives him $70 back, even though he lost. That's the most common surprise: a loser who paid up front gets money back.

Dave won $63, paid $48 for the pizza and had $8 of it, and paid $100 in: $203 from the cash box.

Someone pays a player they didn't play against

Sam owes $52. Instead of paying it all to the cash box, he pays $22 straight to Marcus, and Marcus's line ends with from Sam −$22: Marcus gets $22 less from the cash box. Any loser can pay any winner, as long as the totals match, and House Rules keeps the number of transfers small.

Sam's own transfers have no breakdown, only owed for the session. The line is left out when a player has more than one transfer, as Sam does here, and when the only part is the game result.

A player who paid still owes

The plan only knows payments you recorded. If Sam handed Dave $52 and it isn't in the cash box, Sam still owes it on the screen. Either record the money with Collect in the Cash Box, or mark his transfer as paid, below. See The Cash Box.

Mark a transfer

Tap a transfer that's still open. The payment keys open with the whole amount, here Cash Box → Dave, $203, Full amount. Tap Mark as Paid.

The transfer is crossed out and ticked. From Cash Box says 1 of 5, and the step says 1 of 7 marked.

Marked the wrong one? Tap it again and the tick is gone, as long as the plan hasn't been worked out again since, below. Touch and hold a transfer for more: Mark as Paid, Mark Partial Payment (the same as typing a smaller amount), Mark Paid by Transfer for money that went by bank, and Ask to send a reminder. More in Marking Transfers Done.

The list changed

House Rules works out the plan again when something changes the money: rake or tips, rakeback, expenses, or a partial payment. Transfers you've already marked stay done. They move out of the plan into Already Done, and the new plan counts them.

Sam brings Dave $20 for the cash box, not the whole $30. Dave taps Sam's transfer and types 20: the keys say $10 stays open, and the button becomes Record Payment.

The plan is then worked out again from what's still owed, so the $10 doesn't simply stay on Sam's transfer. Sam still owes $32 in all. The cash box now has $20 more, so it can pay Marcus $49 instead of $29, and Sam owes Marcus only $2 instead of $22. The rest, $30, goes to the cash box. Sam's row counts his $20 in too: Paid $20 of $50, $30 to go. Under Already Done, Dave's $203 and Sam's $20 stay recorded: Done before the plan was recalculated. The plan above already counts them.

So a transfer that "disappeared" was paid before the plan changed: look under Already Done. From then on, its tick is permanent.

Transfers under Already Done can't be unmarked with a tap. A cash box one is undone by deleting its entry in the Cash Box, under Operations: it's the one with the note Settlement: payout from the cash box or Settlement: transfer to the cash box. See The Cash Box Doesn't Match for the entry menu.

A wrong chip value

The chip value turns every chip result into money, so a wrong one makes every transfer wrong by the same factor. If all the amounts look too big or too small, check it: in the session, tap More, the button with three dots at the top right, then Edit Session.

Chip Value is under the blinds, here 1 chip = $0.25. Tap it to change it, then tap the check mark at the top right. Edit Session is only there while the session can still be changed.

A new chip value can be refused if it would make the rake or tips worth less than what's already paid from them: the rakeback, expenses paid from the rake, or tips paid from the cash box.

Fix a finished session

Once a session is finished, nothing in it can be changed. The screenshots here are from last week's game, a different night with five players. It says This session is finished and can't be changed. To fix a mistake, reopen it.

  1. Tap Settlement at the bottom, then Reopen. Or tap the lock button next to Settlement.
  2. House Rules asks Reopen Session?: The settlement will be recalculated. Completed transfers stay. Tap Reopen Session.
  3. A message says Session reopened. Fix the mistake, then finish the session again in Settle Up.

Transfers you marked before stay done and show under Already Done if the fix changes the plan.

Good to know

  • The plan is worked out again in Close Session when the money changes. The Settle Up sheet opened from a session works it out when it opens.
  • Kept by House under the rake step is the part of the rake that isn't rakeback. It's not anyone's transfer.
  • In a session you closed with Close for Now, marking the last open transfer finishes it, and a message says Session settled.
