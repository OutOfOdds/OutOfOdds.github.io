Something Looks Off

The Cash Box Doesn’t Match

House Rules knows how much cash should be in your hands: every payment recorded as cash, minus every payout in cash. Count Cash compares that with what you actually count. When the two differ, the cause is usually a payment recorded the wrong way, a payment you forgot to record, or a payout with the wrong amount. Look for it before you write the difference off.

This page shows a count that comes up short, how one wrong entry caused it, and what to do with a difference you can't explain, short or over.

The example club

All the screenshots come from the Maple Street Game, Dave's home game on Maple Street.

  • The club: Dave hosts for Marcus, Jenna, Tyler, Priya and Sam. They play No-Limit Hold'em at 4/8 blinds with a chip worth $0.25, so a 400-chip buy-in costs $100.
  • Tonight: everyone has cashed out, and nobody has been paid out yet. Five players paid their $100 buy-ins and Marcus and Tyler their $100 rebuys: $700 in the cash box. All of it is recorded as cash except Jenna's $100 by bank transfer, so House Rules expects $600 in cash. Sam hasn't paid and owes $52; money that hasn't come in isn't counted. Dave paid for the $48 pizza from his own pocket, not from the cash box, so it doesn't change the count either.
  • What happens: Dave counts the cash before handing out the winnings and finds $500, not $600. Priya had sent her $100 by bank, but her payment was recorded as cash.

Count the cash

Open the session and tap Cash Box. The tiles split the money by how it came in: $600 in cash and $100 by transfer. Only the cash part is in your hands, so only it is counted. The rake tile is the house's part of the money already in the box: it doesn't change what you count.

Tap Count Cash at the bottom right and type what you counted. Should be in cash is what House Rules expects, Difference is the gap. Dave types $500: the difference is −$100.

Tap the check mark. When the count matches, it's recorded and a message says Cash matches. When it doesn't, a sheet opens with the difference in its title, here $100 Short.

When the cash is short

Find It First lists the players who haven't paid the cash box yet, with what each owes, and a link to the Session Log with every payment and payout of the night. Here Sam really hasn't paid, so that's not it.

What to check, most likely first:

  • A payment recorded as cash that came by bank. The money is in your bank account, not your hands. This is Dave's case.
  • A payout or expense with the wrong amount, or one entered twice.
  • Cash paid out that wasn't recorded, like a winner you paid early or a pizza paid from the cash.
  • Cash that left the room, in a pocket or under the chip tray.

To look through the entries, tap ✕ to close the sheet. Nothing is recorded, not even the count.

Fix the entry

Scroll down to Operations in the Cash Box: every payment, payout and count, newest on top. Touch and hold an entry for its menu:

  • Edit Amount fixes the amount. The cash box can't go below zero, so a change that would leave it short is refused.
  • Mark Paid by Transfer moves a payment from cash to bank, and Mark Paid in Cash moves it back. The cash box total stays the same; only the split between in cash and by transfer changes.
  • Delete removes the entry after asking Delete Operation?

Priya's buy-in payment is the one that came by bank. Dave touches and holds it and chooses Mark Paid by Transfer.

Her line now says payment by transfer, and the tiles show $500 in cash and $200 by transfer.

Dave counts again, types $500 and taps the check mark: Cash matches.

Can't find it

When you've checked everything and the cash is still short, choose what to do with the difference and tap the check mark:

  • Leave It for Now: only notes the count. Nothing changes, and you can count again later.
  • Host Covers It: the host pays the difference. House Rules records a Cash short expense paid from the cash box and puts all of it on the host, so it comes off the host's result. The host is the one named in the club's house rules, see House Rules. With no host there, or one who didn't play tonight, the option says Marked as covered, nothing to split: it only notes the count, like Leave It for Now. The Maple Street Game hasn't named a host, so that's what the screenshot shows.
  • Split Among Players: everyone who played pays an equal share, here $16.67 each, 6 players. It's the same Cash short expense, split evenly. The $16.67 is rounded: the shares add up to exactly $100, with the odd cents on some players.

The Cash short expense shows under Expenses in the Cash Box, and in Settle Up each player's share shows in the line under their transfer as expense share. See Expenses.

These options record an expense paid from the cash box, so there has to be enough money in it. If there isn't, the message says There isn't enough money in the cash box.

When the cash is over

More cash than expected usually means a payment you didn't record: a player paid, but nobody tapped Collect. Record it now and count again. If no player owes anything, the extra may be a rebuy nobody recorded, see The Chips Don't Add Up.

Here, after the count matched, Dave finds a $10 bill under the chip tray and counts $510. The sheet says $10 Over.

When the cash is over, Find It First also lists short chips, when the chips are short and not yet recorded (not the case tonight), with what they're worth: Someone may have paid for a rebuy that isn't recorded. Then the options:

  • Leave It: only notes the count. The extra stays in the cash box.
  • Add to Tips: the extra goes to the dealer tips, here Dealer tips +$10.

Dave picks Add to Tips and taps the check mark. Operations gets an Extra Cash line, added to tips, +$10.

Every count is kept

Each count is a Cash Count line in Operations with the amount you counted and what you did: count, matched, count, $100 short, left for now, count, $10 extra, added to tips, and so on. The amount on a count line is what you counted, not money coming in: a count never changes the cash box total.

Good to know

  • Count Cash is off until the cash box has at least one payment or payout.
  • You can also count in Close Session: on the Collect step, the Cash card has Should be in cash and Count Cash, and says when you last counted. See Close Session, Step by Step.
  • If a payment or payout is added while the count sheet is open, the count is refused: This count doesn't match the cash box anymore. Count again.
  • The cash box can't go below zero. Close Session won't settle up while it is, and says The cash box can't go below zero, with the amount.
  • Marking a settlement transfer as paid in cash adds its payment or payout to the cash box too, so count after the transfers if you settle in cash. See Marking Transfers Done.
  • On the iPad, in the full layout with the sidebar, an entry in the Cash Box next to the session has only Edit Amount and Delete in its menu.
