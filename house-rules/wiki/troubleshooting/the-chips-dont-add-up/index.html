Something Looks Off

The Chips Don’t Add Up

When the last player has cashed out, every chip that was sold has come back: the chips on the table come to zero. If they don't, House Rules stops you before Settle Up and says how far off you are. Chips left over are usually rake and dealer tips you haven't recorded yet. Chips short, more coming back than were sold, usually mean a rebuy nobody recorded.

This page shows both cases, how to find the cause, and what to do when you can't.

The example club

All the screenshots come from the Maple Street Game, Dave's home game on Maple Street.

  • The club: Dave hosts for Marcus, Jenna, Tyler, Priya and Sam. They play No-Limit Hold'em at 4/8 blinds with a chip worth $0.25, so a 400-chip buy-in costs $100.
  • Tonight: six buy-ins and two rebuys are recorded, 3,200 chips sold as far as House Rules knows. Late in the night Priya rebought for 400 chips and gave Dave $100 in cash. Dave recorded the money with Collect, the button in the session's Cash Box for money a player pays in, but forgot to record the rebuy itself. So 3,600 chips were really in play.
  • What happens: Sam is the last one at the table and hands back 944 chips. The table only had 544 left, so 400 chips are short.

Chips short

The cash-out that shows it

Only the last player at the table can cash out more chips than are left. For everyone else, the keys won't take more than the table has. For the last player, the keys warn but let you record what they actually hand back: here 400 chips short. You can sort it out in Close Session. The Left on table line goes to −400.

Record what the player hands back, even when it doesn't fit. Typing a smaller number to make it fit only moves the mistake into their result. Fixing an entry is right only when you know that entry is wrong.

After the last cash-out the session says Everyone Cashed Out with the warning 400 chips short. Record them in Close Session. The tiles show the gap: 3,200 buy-ins, 3,600 cashed out.

Close Session asks about them

Tap Close Session. The Rake and Tips step says 400 chips short, and below the steps there are two cards: Find It First and Who Covers $100. The $100 is the 400 chips at chip value. Settle Up stays locked until the short chips are sorted out: no transfers appear before that.

Find it first: the Session Log

Before anyone pays for the short chips, look for the cause. Tap Session Log under Find It First. You can also open it any time from More, the button with three dots at the top right of the session.

The Session Log lists every buy-in, rebuy, cash-out and cash box payment, newest first. When a player has paid the cash box more than their buy-ins cost, the log says so at the top. Here: Priya paid $100 with no rebuy, with Paid $200, buy-ins cost $100. Add a 400 rebuy? under it.

Tap that line. The rebuy keys open for Priya with 400 already in, and the payment is set to Not Yet, because her $100 is already in the cash box. Picking Cash here would count her money twice.

Tap the check mark. The rebuy goes into the log at the time you add it, not when it happened. Priya's result changes with it: she bought 800 chips and cashed out 548, so she's down 252 chips, −$63, not up $37. And the chips add up: Rake and Tips now says Not needed, and Settle Up is the next step.

The log only spots a payment with no rebuy when the money went into the cash box. Other places to look:

  • A wrong number. Tap the player and look at Entries. Tap an entry, or touch and hold it and choose Edit Amount, to fix it. While chips are short, the check mark only turns on once the fix covers the whole shortage.
  • A rebuy paid outside the cash box, straight to another player or by a bank transfer you didn't record. Ask around the table, then give the player the rebuy with Rebuy.
  • A cash-out recorded too high. An earlier player's cash-out typed with an extra digit still fits on the table, but leaves too few chips for the last player. Check each cash-out against what the player really handed back.

Can't find it: who covers the short chips

If nothing turns up, someone has to pay for the missing chips. In Who Covers $100, choose how to share them, then tap Record Short Chips. Each player's share comes off their result in chips, at chip value. Switch between the options as often as you like: nothing is recorded until you tap the button. Many games agree on this in advance and set it as a house rule, see below.

The screenshots below were taken before the missed rebuy was found, so Priya still shows as a winner. That's one more reason to look first: a wrong cause makes the wrong people pay.

  • Everyone: split equally between all the players. Chips that don't divide go to some players, one each. Here four players cover 67 chips (−$16.75) and two cover 66 (−$16.50).
  • Winners: only players who won chips, in proportion to what they won. Sam won 544 chips and covers 150 (−$37.50), Priya won 148 and covers 41. Losers pay nothing extra. If nobody won chips, the card says Nobody won chips, so everyone covers equally.
  • Host: one player covers all of it. Pick who in Pays. Any player can be chosen, and if the club's house rules name a host who played tonight, they're picked already. Record Short Chips stays off until someone is picked.

The choice starts from the club's Short Chips house rule when it's set, and from Everyone when it isn't. When your choice matches the rule, the card says House Rules under its title. See House Rules.

After Record Short Chips, the step says 400 short chips covered and Settle Up opens. In each covering player's transfer, the line under it shows the share, for example short chips −$16.75. See A Transfer Looks Wrong.

Changing any chips afterwards clears the recorded short chips: a rebuy, a cash-out, an edited or deleted entry, a player brought back in, new rake or tips. The step asks again, with your last choice of who covers. Changing the chip value doesn't clear them. There's no separate button to undo Record Short Chips: when you find the real cause later, record the fix, and the short chips clear by themselves.

Chips left over

The other way round: everyone has cashed out and chips are still on the table. This is another Maple Street night, with no missed rebuy: 3,200 chips were sold and 3,080 came back, so 120 chips, worth $30, are left. The session says No chips should be left on the table (now 120). and a Chips Left on the Table card asks you to assign them to rake and tips before settling up.

Settle Up waits for these too. Tap the card, or in Close Session the Rake and Tips button. The sheet starts with all 120 in Rake and 0 in Tips.

Rake and tips together must use up exactly the chips left over. Until they do, the keys say how far off you are, like 10 not assigned here with 100 in rake and 10 in tips, and the check mark stays off.

No rake or tips tonight? Then the leftover chips mean someone cashed out too little. Check each cash-out in the player's Entries and fix the wrong one with Edit Amount. More about rake and tips is in Rake, Tips and Rakeback.

Good to know

  • Recorded rake and tips can't go below what's already paid from them: the rakeback and expenses paid from the rake, and the tips already paid out of the cash box. Delete those first.
  • The rakeback and the expenses paid from the rake together can't be more than the rake.
  • Short chips are counted in chips, not money: the covering players lose chips from their result, and the chip value turns them into dollars.
  • The Session Log has All, Chips and Cash Box at the top to show only chip entries or only cash box payments.
